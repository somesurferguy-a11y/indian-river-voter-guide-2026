What's on your ballot

Not every race appears on every ballot — your district and party registration determine what you'll see. Each race page states exactly who is eligible to vote in it.

Florida has a closed primary — but that doesn't mean you sit August 18 out

Only voters registered with a party may vote in that party's primary contests. However, nonpartisan races and ballot questions are open to every registered voter, including No Party Affiliation (NPA) voters. In Indian River County that means two contested races anyone can vote on August 18: School Board District 2 (Rosario vs. Lange) and Mosquito Control Seat 2 (Kirkland vs. MacCoy).

Both are decided outright in August — they do not continue to November. The county has about 26,000 registered Democrats and 24,000 NPA voters who have little or nothing else pulling them to the polls that day.

How this guide works

Every claim has a source

Positions, records, votes, and money all carry a link to where the information came from, with a date. If we couldn't verify something, the guide says so rather than filling the gap.

Facts and allegations are labeled differently

A documented vote is not the same as something a political opponent claimed. The guide tags each item as Documented, Vote in office, Allegation — not established, or Unverified, and every allegation carries the candidate's response.

No endorsements, no ranking

This guide does not tell you who to vote for. Candidates appear in ballot order, every candidate in a race gets the same sections, and third-party endorsements are reported as facts about who endorsed — never adopted.
