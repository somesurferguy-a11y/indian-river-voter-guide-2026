Methodology & corrections

A voter guide is only worth as much as its sourcing. Here is exactly how this one is built, what it will and won't publish, and how to get something fixed.

Sourcing

Every factual claim on this site carries a link to its source and the date of that source. Sources are ranked in this order, and the highest available is used:

  1. Primary government records — the Supervisor of Elections, Florida Division of Elections, county and school district minutes and budgets, court filings, campaign finance reports.
  2. Candidates' own words — questionnaires, forum recordings, campaign materials, official statements.
  3. Established local and regional news reporting — TCPalm/Treasure Coast Newspapers, Vero News, Sebastian Daily, WQCS, WPTV, CBS12, Florida Politics.
  4. Partisan or advocacy sources — used only as leads to be corroborated elsewhere, and labeled as partisan wherever cited.

If a claim could not be verified to at least level 3, it does not appear as fact. Where the guide could not find information, it says so explicitly rather than leaving a blank that implies something.

How claims are labeled

The section of each candidate profile covering their record is the part most likely to be misread, so every item is tagged by evidence type:

Documented
Established in a primary source or corroborated reporting. Treat as fact.
Vote in office
A specific recorded vote, with date and outcome, from official minutes or records.
Allegation — not established
Someone made this claim and it has not been proven. Published only when the allegation is itself newsworthy and documented as having been made. Always accompanied by the candidate's response, or an explicit note that no response could be found.
Unverified claim
Circulating publicly but uncorroborated. Included only where it is spreading widely enough that voters benefit from seeing it flagged as unverified.

Media source ratings

Every news citation in this guide carries a small tag showing that outlet's political-lean rating — for example Left-Center. Click any tag to jump back here.

This is not our own judgment. Ratings come from established, published media-bias organizations — chiefly Media Bias/Fact Check and AllSides — not something this guide invented. Where an outlet has no published rating from any of these organizations, the tag says so rather than guessing.

Scope limit: this applies to news/media outlets only. It does not apply to official government records, court filings, campaign websites, or a candidate's own statements — political lean is a media-bias concept, and doesn't meaningfully describe a primary document or a candidate talking about themselves. Those sources carry no tag.

"Not independently rated" means no rating was located from these organizations as of the date checked — most often because the outlet is too small or too new for them to have reviewed it, not because it is neutral or biased in either direction.

On "opposition research"

This guide researches every candidate's public record with equal effort — that includes material a candidate would rather voters didn't see. That is ordinary accountability journalism, and it is applied evenly:

  • Equal effort, disclosed. The same searches are run against every candidate in a race regardless of party. Where a search turned up little, the guide says so, rather than leaving an empty section that reads as a clean bill of health.
  • Lawsuits are not verdicts. A filed complaint is an accusation. Where a case is cited, the guide reports its outcome — dismissed, settled, pending — or states that the outcome could not be determined.
  • Right of reply is built in. No allegation publishes without the candidate's response alongside it.
  • Relevance test. Items are included when they bear on fitness for the office sought: official conduct, use of public money, honesty in public statements, legal and professional history, and who funds the campaign. Family members, private medical matters, and material with no bearing on the office are out of scope.
  • Legal contributions are not scandals. Campaign finance is reported as amounts and donor identities so readers can draw their own conclusions. Only actual reported violations or fines are characterized as such.

Neutrality

  • This guide makes no endorsement and does not rank, score, or recommend candidates.
  • Candidates appear in ballot order where known, otherwise alphabetically by surname. Order implies nothing.
  • Every candidate in a race receives the same sections, whether or not there was material to fill them.
  • Third-party endorsements are reported as facts about who endorsed whom — never adopted as this guide's view.
  • The visual design deliberately avoids red/blue coding so no candidate gets extra weight.

Known limitations

Read this before relying on the guide.

  • Campaign finance goes stale fast. Figures reflect the most recent report retrieved, and the "as of" date is shown with every total. Newer filings may exist.
  • Coverage is uneven by race. Down-ballot races get far less press than the County Commission, so there is genuinely less to report on those candidates. The contested Mosquito Control race in particular has almost no available information.
  • State and federal races are thinner than local ones. Candidate names and ballot status for the U.S. Senate, U.S. House District 9 and Florida House District 34 are confirmed from official Division of Elections filings, but documented positions are sparse, and their campaign finance (filed with the state and FEC rather than the county) was not retrieved for this edition.
  • Incumbents have longer records. A challenger's shorter record section reflects a shorter public career, not superior conduct.
  • Deadlines and ballot content are summarized, not authoritative. Always confirm with the Supervisor of Elections at (772) 226-4700 before acting.

Corrections

Errors in a voter guide are worse than errors elsewhere, because people act on them. Any candidate, campaign, or reader may request a correction, and requests are handled the same way regardless of who sends them.

  • Factual errors are corrected as fast as they can be verified, and the page notes what changed and when.
  • Candidates may submit a position statement for any issue currently marked "No position found," and it will be added with attribution.
  • Candidates may respond to any item in their record section, and the response will be published alongside it.
  • Disagreement with a sourced fact is not grounds for removal, but a documented rebuttal will be published next to it.

Independence

This is an independent volunteer project. It is not affiliated with, funded by, or coordinated with any candidate, campaign, political party, political committee, or government agency. It accepts no advertising and no contributions from anyone with a stake in these races.
