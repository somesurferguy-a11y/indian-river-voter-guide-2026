# Genealogy research

Research that holds up: to a cousin who disagrees, to a lineage society, to a
certification judge, to yourself in ten years.

## The standard being applied

Every conclusion is built to the **Genealogical Proof Standard**:

1. reasonably exhaustive research;
2. complete and accurate source citations;
3. thorough analysis and correlation of the collected information;
4. resolution of conflicting evidence;
5. a soundly reasoned, coherently written conclusion.

A conclusion that fails any one of the five is not proved. Say so rather than
hedging - `references/documentation-standards.md` gives the self-audit.

## Vocabulary - use it exactly

- **Source** -> *original* / *derivative* / *authored narrative*.
- **Information** -> *primary* / *secondary* / *undetermined*, assessed **per
  assertion**, not per document. One document routinely carries both.
- **Evidence** -> *direct* / *indirect* / *negative*. Evidence is a relationship
  between an information item and a **research question**, so the same item is
  direct for one question and indirect for another.
- **Negative evidence** (absence where presence was expected) is not a **negative
  search result** (a search that found nothing). Always say which you mean.
- **Proof** -> *proof statement* / *proof summary* / *proof argument*.
- Say **conclusion** or **assertion**, never "fact".

## Non-negotiables

- **Never invent an identifier.** Not a page number, film number, volume, URL,
  catalog number, record-set name, or standard number. If it is not verified, use
  a slot the user fills from the record in front of them. A wrong number is worse
  than no number, because a reader cannot tell which parts to trust.
- **Cite what was actually used**, not what one wishes had been used. An index is
  not the record. A digital image of a register is not a transcript of it. Say
  which layer was examined.
- **Record the jurisdiction as it was at the moment of the event**, plus the
  present-day equivalent, plus the level of government that held the record.
- **A stated relationship is an assertion by an informant.** Identify the
  informant, ask what they could have known, and ask what they wanted.
- **Do not delete losing evidence.** A conflict is resolved in writing, with both
  sides stated at their strongest, or it is recorded as unresolved.
- **DNA is probabilistic.** A shared amount gives a *range* of possible
  relationships, never one relationship. It supports a hypothesis only inside a
  documentary framework, and it excludes more decisively than it confirms.
- **Living people have privacy interests**, and a DNA tester has them in their own
  body's information. Consent to test is not consent to publish.

## Working posture

Correlate before concluding. When a conclusion is asked for and the evidence does
not carry it, give the honest alternative: the best-supported hypothesis, what
would confirm or refute it, and the specific next records to search.
