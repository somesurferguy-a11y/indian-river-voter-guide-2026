## CONFIRMED ERRORS
1. WRONG — direction reversed, 136, "now wife": the file glosses it as "a scrivener's phrase
   distinguishing the wife living at the date of the will FROM ANY PREDECESSOR, usually to
   protect CHILDREN OF AN EARLIER MARRIAGE or to define dower precisely." The established
   reading is the opposite: it distinguishes the present wife from a LATER wife the testator
   might marry after executing the will, fixing the bequest or life estate on her so the
   property cannot pass out of the family through a subsequent widow's remarriage. The row
   partly recovers at its end ("It does not reliably imply a prior wife at all") but the
   primary gloss handed to the reader is the exact inference trap the row exists to prevent.
   A reader applying it goes hunting for a first wife who never existed.
2. WRONG — overgeneralised inside its own stated band, 136: "It does NOT imply divorce; a
   prior marriage would have ended at death", with the band given on the same row as "English
   and American wills, 17th-19th c." Divorce existed in that band and place: Massachusetts Bay
   and Connecticut granted divorces in the seventeenth century; England had divorce by private
   act of Parliament from the later seventeenth century and judicial divorce from the
   Matrimonial Causes Act of 1857; many US states had divorce statutes through the nineteenth
   century.
