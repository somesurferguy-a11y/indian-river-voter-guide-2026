## Background: Is Flock/ALPR a live local issue in Indian River County?

Yes — very live and topical as of this research (Aug 2026). Indian River County Sheriff Eric Flowers
publicly credited the county's Flock Safety automated license-plate-reader (ALPR) network with helping
catch a homicide suspect (Matthew Waggle, fled to West Virginia after the killing of Dawn Kriskewic in
Vero Beach). County operates roughly 140 license plate readers. Vero Beach PD separately expanded its
own camera network (Vero News, Sept. 8, 2025: "Vero cops expand surveillance with hi-tech cameras").
Community reaction is described as "divided" — supporters cite crime-solving value, the ACLU and privacy
advocates raise concerns about tracking without probable cause, data retention, and use for immigration
enforcement (Martin/St. Lucie/Indian River county agencies reportedly ran 216 immigration-related Flock
searches in a 3-month window in early 2026 per CBS12 reporting). Sources:
- [Community divided over Flock Safety cameras after Treasure Coast homicide arrest, WFLX, Aug. 23, 2026](https://www.wflx.com/2026/08/23/community-divided-over-flock-safety-cameras-after-treasure-coast-homicide-arrest/)
- [Vero cops expand surveillance with hi-tech cameras, Vero News, Sept. 8, 2025](https://veronews.com/2025/09/08/vero-cops-expand-surveillance-with-hi-tech-cameras/)
- [Flock Cameras helped capture IRC murder suspect, but privacy debate continues, CBS12](https://cbs12.com/news/local/flock-cameras-helped-capture-indian-river-county-murder-suspect-fled-florida-west-virginia-murder-suspect-matthew-waggle-victim-dawn-kriskewic-indian-river-county-sheriffs-office-florida-news)
- [As Flock cameras fuel privacy debate, local police seek access to even more video, CBS12](https://cbs12.com/news/local/flock-cameras-fuel-privacy-debate-local-police-seek-access-doorbell-cameras-security-cameras-st-lucie-county-sheriff-martin-county-sheriff-west-palm-beach-police-privacy-concerns-law-enforcement-tool)

No named Vero Beach City Council member, county commissioner, school board member, mosquito control
board member, or Fellsmere council member has been found (as of this research) taking a public vote or
statement specifically on adopting/expanding Flock cameras. The Sheriff (Eric Flowers, not on our
candidate list — his office runs the county ALPR program) and municipal police chiefs (e.g., Vero Beach
Police Chief David Currey) are the officials quoted supporting the technology; no candidate-specific
statements located yet per-candidate research below.

**2. Data centers/AI infrastructure:** This is a genuinely live local regulatory issue, but neither
candidate has been quoted on it. On July 14, 2026, Indian River County Planning & Development Services
Director John Stoll issued a written determination that data centers are **currently a prohibited use**
under county land development regulations (citing high electrical demand, backup generators, and
water-intensive cooling systems as reasons they don't fit any existing zoning category). County staff
say they are evaluating regulatory changes in response to new state law (Florida SB 484, 2026, which
revises regulation of large-scale data centers and major electricity users), but "no timeline has been
established" and no Board of County Commissioners vote has yet occurred. The only official quoted is
Kathy Copeland (county Legislative Affairs & Communications Manager), not any commissioner or candidate.
Because Loar currently chairs the Board that will eventually have to vote on any zoning change, this is
worth monitoring but there is **no recorded position from Loar or Jaffe** as of this research.
[Indian River County: No Path Yet for Data Centers, WQCS, Aug. 4, 2026](https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2026-08-04/indian-river-county-no-path-yet-for-data-centers?_amp=true).
