## AMENDMENT 3 — Save Our Homes from Excessive Property Taxes
*(This is the measure the guide has already documented separately. Included here only for cross-reference completeness and to add campaign-finance and argument detail found in this research pass. Do not duplicate in the final guide.)*

**Already documented elsewhere:** homestead exemption $50K → $150K (2027) → $250K (2028); non-school homestead property taxes eliminated by 2037; Senate 30-9, House 75-26; statewide fiscal impact $4.6B rising to $8.4B/year.

### Additional detail found in this pass

**OFFICIAL BALLOT TITLE AND SUMMARY — quoted verbatim** (Source: [Ballotpedia, Amendment 3 page](https://ballotpedia.org/Florida_Amendment_3,_Homestead_Tax_Exemptions,_Property_Assessments,_and_Spending_Restrictions_Amendment_(2026)), accessed Aug 8, 2026, reproducing the joint-resolution ballot language):

> **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT — ARTICLE VII, SECTIONS 4, 6, AND 9; ARTICLE XII**
> **SAVE OUR HOMES FROM EXCESSIVE PROPERTY TAXES.** — This amendment benefits Florida taxpayers by:
> Exempting homestead properties from taxation. Exempts the first $250,000 of a homestead's value from taxation for all levies other than school district levies and requires, through general law, a schedule for full elimination.
> Ensuring funding for core services. Requires local governments to use remaining property taxes solely for core public needs including public safety, education and schools, infrastructure, and natural resources.
> Protecting small businesses. Limits future property tax assessments on businesses.
> Ensuring fairness for Florida residents. Requires any person who establishes Florida residency after January 1, 2027, to maintain Florida residency for five years prior to receiving the increased homestead exemption.
> If approved, the amendment would take effect on January 1, 2027.

**Provisions not obvious from the ballot title (VERIFIED FACT, Ballotpedia measure page):**
- Non-homestead property (rentals, commercial buildings, second homes): annual **assessment-increase cap drops from 10% to 5%**, for non-school levies.
- New residents establishing Florida residency after Jan 1, 2027 receive a **smaller exemption until they have lived in Florida five years**.
- The $250,000 figure is **indexed to inflation beginning 2029**.
- Counties and municipalities are **restricted in how they may spend remaining property tax revenue** — to public safety, education/schools, infrastructure, natural resource projects and flood control, local bonds, employee retirement benefits, and government operations.

## AMENDMENT 1 — Budget Stabilization Fund
*(Number REPORTED, not yet confirmed against Secretary of State certified ballot. Ballotpedia title: "Florida Changes to Budget Stabilization Fund Amendment (2026)".)*

### 1. OFFICIAL BALLOT TITLE AND SUMMARY — quoted verbatim
Source: [Ballotpedia, Florida Changes to Budget Stabilization Fund Amendment (2026)](https://ballotpedia.org/Florida_Changes_to_Budget_Stabilization_Fund_Amendment_(2026)), reproducing the text of HJR 5019; accessed Aug 8, 2026.

> **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT — ARTICLE III, SECTION 19**
> **BUDGET STABILIZATION FUND.** — Proposing an amendment to the State Constitution to increase the amount of funds that may be retained in the budget stabilization fund from 10% to 25% of general revenue collections, require the legislature to transfer the lesser of $750 million or the amount required to reach 25% of the general revenue collections each year unless certain conditions are met, and allow the legislature to withdraw funds for critical state needs.

### 2. PLAIN ENGLISH — what it actually does
Florida has a state savings account, created by voters in 1992, called the **Budget Stabilization Fund** — the "rainy day fund." Today the constitution caps it at **10%** of the prior year's net general revenue, and the constitution says nothing about putting money in each year (that is left to ordinary state law).

This amendment would:
- **Raise the cap** on the fund from 10% to **25%** of net general revenue.
- **Require an annual deposit** written into the constitution: either **$750 million**, or whatever smaller amount is needed to hit the 25% cap — whichever is less.
- **Let the Legislature skip the deposit** in two situations: (a) no more than **once every five years**, if two-thirds of the Legislature votes that there is a "critical state need" costing more than the deposit would be; or (b) if the Legislature withdrew money from the fund in the same fiscal year.
- **Set the rules for taking money out.** The Legislature could withdraw for (a) an emergency, (b) covering a general-revenue shortfall, or (c) a "critical state need" — but only if the fund balance is above 15% of revenue collections and two-thirds of the Legislature approves.

Context (VERIFIED FACT, Ballotpedia): in FY 2024-25 the fund held **9.3%** of state revenue. Since FY 1994-95 the fund has averaged **4.63%** of net general revenue. Florida last drew down more than it deposited in **2008**, during the Great Recession.

### 3. YES vs. NO — stated symmetrically
- **A YES vote** means you want the state constitution changed to raise the rainy-day fund cap from 10% to 25%, require the roughly $750 million annual deposit, and set the new rules for suspending deposits and making withdrawals.
- **A NO vote** means you want the constitution left as it is: the rainy-day fund stays capped at 10%, no annual deposit is constitutionally required, and the current withdrawal rules stay in place.

### 4. Who put it on the ballot — VERIFIED FACT
**Legislature**, via **House Joint Resolution 5019 (2025)**. Filed June 4, 2025.
- **Florida House, June 16, 2025: 100 Yes – 1 No** (19 not voting). Democrats 23-0; Republicans 77-1. Threshold was 72.
- **Florida Senate, June 16, 2025: 29 Yes – 4 No** (6 not voting). Democrats 4-4; Republicans 24-0; Independent 1-0. Threshold was 23 (of members voting basis; 60% requirement).
- Ballotpedia classifies the vote as **bipartisan** (Partisan Direction Index +11.9%).
Source: Ballotpedia measure page, "Path to the ballot," accessed Aug 8, 2026.

### 5. Campaign funding — VERIFIED FACT
**Ballotpedia has located no committee registered either in support of or in opposition to this amendment.** Support $0.00 / Oppose $0.00 as of reports processed. (Ballotpedia's finance note on this page reflects reports covering through **July 14, 2025** — this page's finance data is stale relative to the Amendment 3 page. Treat as "no known committee" rather than a current dollar figure.)

### 6. Arguments FOR and AGAINST — named
**FOR:**
- **Senate President Ben Albritton (R-27):** "[The amendment] makes Florida's balance sheet more durable in difficult times, and we're setting more money aside to have its rainy-day reserves if things get difficult."
- **House Speaker Daniel Perez (R-116):** "I think this is just a preparation of being prepared for the unexpected... we have a sample to look at in the 2000s when we had a recession. The state of Florida was not prepared for that recession."
- Also listed as supporters: **State Rep. Lawrence McClure (R)**.

**AGAINST:**

- Ballotpedia has located **no organized opposition campaign**.

### 7. Official fiscal impact statement
**GAP — NOT LOCATED.** Under Florida law the **Financial Impact Estimating Conference** prepares financial impact statements for **citizen initiative** amendments. Legislatively referred amendments such as this one are accompanied instead by **legislative staff analyses**, not an FIEC statement. No FIEC financial impact statement for HJR 5019 was located in this research pass. See "Gaps."
What is known: the deposit requirement redirects up to **$750 million/year** of state general revenue into the reserve fund rather than into recurring appropriations. This is a **state** general-revenue effect; it does not directly touch local property taxes.

### 8. 60% threshold
**VERIFIED FACT.** Requires **60% approval** (Art. XI, § 5, Fla. Const.).

### 9. Effect on Indian River County / local government revenue
**No direct effect on Indian River County revenue.** This amendment governs the **state** general revenue fund only. Indirect and unquantified: money set aside in the state reserve is money not available for state appropriations, which can include state aid programs that flow to counties and school districts. No source located that quantifies an Indian River County-specific impact. **UNVERIFIED as to indirect local effect.**

## AMENDMENT 2 — Exemption of Tangible Personal Property on Agricultural Land from Taxation
*(Number REPORTED, not yet confirmed against Secretary of State certified ballot. Ballotpedia title: "Florida Exempt Tangible Personal Property Used for Agriculture or Agritourism from Property Taxes Amendment (2026)".)*

### 1. OFFICIAL BALLOT TITLE AND SUMMARY — quoted verbatim
Source: [Ballotpedia measure page](https://ballotpedia.org/Florida_Exempt_Tangible_Personal_Property_Used_for_Agriculture_or_Agritourism_from_Property_Taxes_Amendment_(2026)), reproducing the text of HJR 1215; accessed Aug 8, 2026.

> **CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT — ARTICLE VII, SECTION 3; ARTICLE XII**
> **EXEMPTION OF TANGIBLE PERSONAL PROPERTY ON AGRICULTURAL LAND FROM TAXATION.** — Proposing an amendment to the State Constitution to exempt tangible personal property habitually located or typically present on land classified as agricultural, used in the production of agricultural products or for agritourism activities, and owned by the landowner or leaseholder of the agricultural land from ad valorem taxation. If approved this amendment would first apply for tax years beginning January 1, 2027.

**Operative constitutional text to be added (verbatim, new Art. VII, § 3(h)):**
> (h)(1) Tangible personal property that meets all of the following conditions shall be exempt from ad valorem taxation:
> a. Habitually located or typically present on land classified as agricultural.
> b. Used in the production of agricultural products or for agritourism activities.
> c. Owned by the landowner or leaseholder of the agricultural land.
> (2) The exemption provided by this subsection is subject to conditions and limitations and reasonable definitions as specified by the legislature in general law.

### 2. PLAIN ENGLISH — what it actually does
"Tangible personal property" (TPP) means physical, movable business assets — machinery, tools, equipment. In Florida, counties and other local governments (not the state) tax business TPP, with the **first $25,000 of value per taxpayer already exempt**.

This amendment would **fully exempt farm and agritourism equipment from local property tax** — with no dollar cap — if the equipment is (a) usually kept on land that carries an **agricultural classification**, (b) used to produce agricultural products or run agritourism activities, and (c) owned by the person who owns or leases that agricultural land.

It also gives the **Legislature power to define and limit the exemption by ordinary statute** — so exactly which equipment qualifies would be filled in later by lawmakers, not by the amendment itself.

If approved, it **first applies to tax years beginning January 1, 2027**.

Note: Florida agricultural **land** already gets a favorable "greenbelt" assessment based on use value rather than market value. This amendment addresses the **equipment**, which is separately taxed.

### 3. YES vs. NO — stated symmetrically
- **A YES vote** means you want the constitution changed so that farm and agritourism equipment kept on agriculturally classified land is exempt from local property tax starting with tax year 2027, with the Legislature setting the details.
- **A NO vote** means you want the constitution left as it is: farm equipment continues to be taxed as tangible personal property by local governments, subject to the existing $25,000 per-taxpayer exemption.

### 4. Who put it on the ballot — VERIFIED FACT
**Legislature**, via **House Joint Resolution 1215 (2025)**.
- **Florida House, April 25, 2025: 110 Yes – 1 No** (8 not voting). Democrats 30-1; Republicans 80-0. Threshold 72.
- **Florida Senate, May 2, 2025: 37 Yes – 0 No** (1 not voting). Democrats 10-0; Republicans 27-0.
- Ballotpedia classifies the vote as **bipartisan** (PDI +2.4%); near-unanimous.
Source: Ballotpedia measure page, "Path to the ballot," accessed Aug 8, 2026.

### 9. Effect on Indian River County / local government revenue
**Direct but unquantified local effect.** Because TPP tax is levied by **counties, municipalities, school districts, and special districts** — not the state — the entire revenue loss falls on **local** governments. Indian River County has substantial citrus, cattle and row-crop acreage west of I-95 and along the Fellsmere corridor, plus a growing agritourism sector, so the county is likely to see a proportionally larger revenue effect than urban counties. **No Indian River County-specific dollar estimate was located.** Property Appraiser's office would be the authoritative local source. Labeled **UNVERIFIED** as to local dollar amount.
