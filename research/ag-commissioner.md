## Office overview

The Commissioner of Agriculture sits on the Florida Cabinet and runs the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) — 12 divisions and seven offices regulating farm products, animal/aquaculture/forestry/produce industries, licensing, state forests, wildfire response, concealed-weapons licensing, the hemp industry, and consumer-fraud/weights-and-measures enforcement. FDACS oversees a roughly $13 billion agricultural sector (estimated $213+ billion with economic multiplier effects).
Source: [WLRN, "Election 2026: Florida's primary races for commissioner of agriculture," Aug. 10, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-10/election-2026-floridas-primary-races-for-commissioner-of-agriculture)

## REPUBLICAN PRIMARY

### Wilton Simpson (incumbent) — VERIFIED FACTS unless noted

- Born June 28, 1966, Lakeland, FL; began working on his father's egg farm in Trilby (Pasco County) as a teenager; built a large-scale egg-laying operation supplying Florida grocery stores, plus Simpson Environmental Services and Simpson Farms. Operation now houses roughly 1 million chickens. Net worth $31.5 million as of Dec. 31 (per state financial disclosure).
 Source: [WLRN, Aug. 10, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-10/election-2026-floridas-primary-races-for-commissioner-of-agriculture); [Wikipedia, "Wilton Simpson," accessed 2026-08-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_Simpson)
- Florida Senate 2012–2022 (District 18); Majority Leader 2016–2018; Senate President 2020–2022. As Senate President, backed the Florida Wildlife Corridor Act (2021, ~8 million acres protected) and school-choice expansion (HB 7045, 2022).
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Wilton Simpson," accessed 2026-08-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_Simpson)
- Won the open Ag Commissioner seat outright in the Nov. 2022 general election with 59.3% over Democrat Naomi Blemur; took office Jan. 3, 2023, as the 13th Commissioner of Agriculture. (Not an appointment — see correction above.)
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Wilton Simpson," accessed 2026-08-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_Simpson)

**2025 immigration feud with Gov. DeSantis — VERIFIED FACT, full timeline:**

- Jan. 27–28, 2025: In defiance of DeSantis (who had called his own special session), the GOP-controlled Legislature convened its own third special session and passed SB 2-B, the "Tackling and Reforming Unlawful Migration Policy (TRUMP) Act" — Senate 21-16, House 82-30. Sponsors: Rep. Lawrence McClure and Sen. Joe Gruters. The bill designated Simpson as the state's sole liaison to federal immigration authorities, created an "Office of State Immigration Enforcement" within FDACS's Division of Law Enforcement, and appropriated $515,030,239, including a $500M local-law-enforcement reimbursement grant program to be run through FDACS.
 Source: [Florida Senate, Bill CS/SB 2-B history](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025B/2B); [Florida Phoenix, Jan. 28, 2025](https://floridaphoenix.com/2025/01/28/florida-legislature-pushes-through-immigration-bill-over-desantis-objections/)
- DeSantis publicly opposed putting Simpson in the role. Quote (Jan. 28, 2025, tele-town hall): "Agriculture has not exactly been known for immigration enforcement, so it's almost like the fox guarding the hen house. It was bizarre." He demanded tougher provisions instead: mandatory local-ICE cooperation, blocking wire transfers abroad, a new state crime for illegal entry, and mandatory death penalty for capital crimes committed by undocumented immigrants.
 Source: [Fox 13 Tampa Bay, Jan. 28, 2025](https://www.fox13news.com/news/undocumented-immigration-bill-has-floridas-governor-agriculture-commissioner-legislation-odds)
- Simpson pushed back (Jan. 28-29, 2025): "There's a lot of people who like to beat up on farmers when it's convenient for them, but most of the time they're doing it with a full belly." He noted that (per his own figures) only about 4% of Florida's undocumented workforce is in agriculture, with construction and housekeeping employing more. He also took a jab implying DeSantis's opposition was politically motivated, referencing DeSantis's failed 2024 presidential run: "I'm not the one who opposed and ran against President Trump."
 Source: [Fox 13 Tampa Bay, Jan. 28, 2025](https://www.fox13news.com/news/undocumented-immigration-bill-has-floridas-governor-agriculture-commissioner-legislation-odds); [floridapolitics.com, Jan. 2025](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/718058-desantis-simpson-fox-henhouse/)
- Wikipedia additionally notes DeSantis criticized Simpson over Simpson's past support (as a state senator) for a 2014 bill granting in-state college tuition to children brought to the U.S. illegally — cited by DeSantis as evidence Simpson was soft on immigration.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Wilton Simpson," accessed 2026-08-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_Simpson)
- Feb. 3-4, 2025: DeSantis softened his tone: "We've had great discussions. I think we'll land the plane." Negotiations continued.
 Source: [WFSU, Feb. 4, 2025](https://news.wfsu.org/state-news/2025-02-04/gov-desantis-takes-a-conciliatory-tone-on-floridas-immigration-bill)
- Feb. 13, 2025: DeSantis signed a separate, compromise immigration package (not SB 2-B) that funded ~$298M for 50+ new immigration-focused law enforcement officers, created a new state crime for illegal entry, and mandated the death penalty for capital crimes by undocumented immigrants — but did NOT make Simpson the state's chief immigration officer. Coverage at the time noted lawmakers "did away with a previous proposal to strip the governor of much of his emergency powers on immigration," and separately that the plan to make Simpson chief immigration officer was scrapped in this compromise round.
 Source: [WLRN, Feb. 13, 2025](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2025-02-13/desantis-signs-sweeping-immigration-laws-for-trumps-agenda)
- Formal disposition of the original SB 2-B (the bill that would have empowered Simpson): presented to the Governor Feb. 20, 2025, and vetoed. The veto message was referred Feb. 27, 2025, and formally transmitted to the Secretary of State July 17, 2025 (a procedural/paperwork date, not a substantive later event).
 Source: [Florida Senate, Bill CS/SB 2-B history](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025B/2B)
- **Net outcome:** DeSantis prevailed — Simpson did not end up with statutory chief-immigration-officer authority. The episode is nonetheless documented (by Wikipedia and multiple Florida outlets) as "widely described as a feud" between Simpson and DeSantis.

**Continued immigration commentary (2025), relevant to farmworker/labor policy:**
