# Vero Beach City Council — Former-Member Records Research (Kramer, Heady, Zudans)
Research date: Aug 26, 2026

## Summary

The prior research pass's claim that "no council votes possible... none has held this office" for the six non-incumbent 2026 candidates **did not hold up for two of them**, not just Kramer. **Jay Kramer** served roughly six years on Vero Beach City Council (Nov. 2010–Nov. 2016) with two terms as Mayor (2010–2011, 2015–2016), including a highly-documented reversal on the Vero Electric/FPL sale question, a failed FP&L-rate-increase resolution, and — separately from his council record but directly relevant to identity-verification — is confirmed as the *same* Jay Kramer who chairs the Indian River County Republican Executive Committee and who FBI agents sought to question in Jan. 2021 about his attendance at the "Save America" rally preceding the Capitol riot. **Brian Heady also actually served one term on council (2009–2011)** — the existing guide entry undersold this by calling him only a "perennial candidate," which is true of his 25 *other* campaigns but not accurate about his one win; his single term included a self-reversal on the FPL sale vote and a lawsuit he filed against the city while still in office. **Tracey Zudans** picks up one clearly new, well-sourced item beyond her existing recusal entry: she was the lone dissenting vote against the city's FY2023-24 budget/millage increase in Sept./Oct. 2023, and fuller detail is now available on the June 7, 2024 Three Corners/SuDa recusal (exact vote tallies, the identity of the person who sent the pressuring email, and the underlying May 28, 2024 SuDa vote) that sharpens what the guide currently states only vaguely. Net effect: the "equal-depth" asymmetry the prior pass described as "inherent" was partly an artifact of insufficient research, not just of Moore/Vos being sitting officeholders — this guide should now carry real record sections for Kramer and Heady, not empty ones.

## JAY KRAMER — Vero Beach City Council / two-time Mayor (Nov. 2010 – Nov. 2016)

### Bio verification (the guide's existing claim)
- **Confirmed accurate.** Kramer served as a Vero Beach City Council member from Nov. 15, 2010 to Nov. 4, 2016, and was selected by his council colleagues as Mayor for two terms: 2010–2011 and 2015–2016. Type: fact. Sources: Vero News, "Vero Beach City Council to be sworn in, select mayor today," Nov. 8, 2010, https://veronews.com/2010/11/08/vero-beach-city-council-to-be-sworn-in-select-mayor-today/amp/; Vero Beach 32963, "Will new Mayor Kramer steer Vero Council back to the right?," Dec. 3, 2015, https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202015/DECEMBER%202015/VB32963_Will_New_Mayor_Kramer_Steer_Vero_Council_Back_To_The_Right_Issue49_120315.html; corroborated by his own LinkedIn profile (self-reported, so given less weight standing alone), https://www.linkedin.com/in/votejaykramer/, and by his 2016 county-commission campaign-finance candidate record on the county's VoterFocus system, https://www.voterfocus.com/CampaignFinance/candidate_pr.php?op=cv&e=13&c=indian&ca=312&rellevel=4&committee=N (fetched Aug. 26, 2026), which independently lists him as "existing City of Vero Beach Council Member since 2010" and mayor 2010-2011/2015-2016.
- I did not find a direct, independent (non-Kramer-sourced) confirmation of the exact Nov. 4, 2016 end date for his council seat, nor explicit reporting on whether he declined to seek re-election to the council in 2016 or was simply not on that year's council ballot because he was instead running for County Commission. Flagged as a minor gap — the *fact* that his council/mayoral service ended around Nov. 2016 is well-corroborated across sources; the precise mechanism (retired vs. term-limited vs. chose county race instead) is not.

### Vero Electric / FPL sale reversal — his defining council-era issue (2010–2014)
- **Type: fact/vote.** Kramer was elected in 2010 as part of a slate of candidates who favored selling the city's municipal electric utility (Vero Electric) to Florida Power & Light. He subsequently reversed himself and became the leading council opponent of the sale, arguing the city could run the utility without a power plant by upgrading transmission capacity or shedding county customers. This made him, by 2012, the target of "pro-sale fundamentalists" criticism (their term, not his) and Tea Party criticism from then-Commissioner Bob Solari for being insufficiently pro-sale-conservative going into their eventual 2016 primary matchup. Source: Indian River Guardian (opinion/advocacy outlet, pro-Kramer editorial slant — treat framing with caution, but the underlying factual sequence of his reversal is corroborated elsewhere), "UPDATE: Time is proving Kramer right, Solari, Faherty and other pro-sale fundamentalists wrong," Oct. 14, 2014, https://indianriverguardian.com/2014/10/14/update-time-is-proving-kramer-right-solari-faherty-and-other-pro-sale-fundamentalists-wrong/; corroborated (without the editorializing) by Vero Beach 32963, Dec. 3, 2015, cited above.
- **Type: vote (failed motion).** In early 2013, Kramer proposed a council resolution opposing an FPL rate-increase request, framing it as being about lowering rates generally rather than targeting the city specifically; the resolution was rejected by the then-council majority: Mayor Pilar Turner, Vice Mayor Craig Fletcher, and Councilwoman Tracy Carroll. Source: Vero Beach 32963 search-indexed coverage cited via web search of "Jay Kramer" council votes; I was not able to independently re-fetch the specific dated article for a direct quote, so this is reported as an aggregated-search-result fact rather than a directly-read primary source — flagged for a follow-up direct read before treating the exact vote count as final.
- **Type: fact.** On March 12, 2013, Vero Beach residents voted in a referendum to approve FPL's proposed acquisition of the municipal electric system; the sale closed in 2014, and the full transition (including the last Vero-served customers) was completed by Dec. 2018 — after Kramer had left the council. Source: SEC/FPL filings and Vero News, "Vero to FPL: 'It's finally all yours,'" Dec. 20, 2018, http://veronews.com/2018/12/20/vero-fpl-finally/.

### Republican Executive Committee chairmanship — identity confirmed
- **This resolves the guide's open question.** The guide currently states: "a Jay Kramer has separately been reported as an officer of the Indian River Republican Executive Committee in past coverage; this guide has not confirmed whether that is the same person." **It is the same person, confirmed directly.** Vero News's Jan. 28, 2021 article identifies him in the same sentence as both "former Mayor Jay Kramer" and "the current Indian River County Republican Executive Committee Chairman." Type: fact. Source: Vero News, "FBI agents seek to question former Mayor Jay Kramer," Jan. 28, 2021, http://veronews.com/2021/01/28/fbi-agents-seek-to-question-former-mayor-jay-kramer/. Independently corroborated by a 2012 article establishing his path into state GOP politics (Vero News, "Vero Beach's Jay Kramer seeking state committee post," Aug. 6, 2012, https://veronews.com/2012/08/06/vero-beachs-jay-kramer-seeking-state-committee-post/) and a 2020 article referring to him as "GOP leader Jay Kramer" while covering his wife's school board candidacy (Vero Beach 32963, "GOP leader Jay Kramer's wife in school board race," June 18, 2020, https://www.vb32963online.com/STORIES%202020/JUNE%202020/VB32963_GOP_Leader_Jay_Kramers_Wife_In_School_Board_Race_Issue25_061820.html).

### FBI contact re: Jan. 6, 2021 rally attendance
- **Type: fact, with an allegation-adjacent element Kramer directly responded to.** Two FBI agents (one identified as Richard Miller, Fort Pierce office) visited Kramer's home seeking to question him about his attendance at Trump's "Save America" rally in Washington, D.C. on Jan. 6, 2021, after he posted (then deleted) a social-media photo of himself with the Capitol visible in the background. **Kramer's response, on the record:** he refused to speak with the agents ("You work for the prosecution, and I have a right to remain silent"), stated he "absolutely did not participate in the violent and deadly insurrection," said he found out about the Capitol breach while driving home, disputed that the rally crowd he personally saw was violent, and blamed the riot on Antifa and Democratic allies rather than Trump supporters. He retained legal counsel and notified state GOP leadership of the FBI contact. No report of any charge, indictment, or further law-enforcement action against him was found in this search. Source: Vero News, "FBI agents seek to question former Mayor Jay Kramer," Jan. 28, 2021, http://veronews.com/2021/01/28/fbi-agents-seek-to-question-former-mayor-jay-kramer/.

### REC censure controversy — Barefoot, Sept. 2021
- **Type: fact/allegation, with response.** As REC Chairman, Kramer announced the committee's censure of then–School Board Chairman Brian Barefoot for supporting a school mask mandate against Gov. DeSantis's order. When Barefoot publicly asked Kramer what "censure" actually meant procedurally, Kramer could not define it on the spot ("That would be a definition to the membership of the REC"), and Barefoot mocked the action ("Do I get a plaque or something?"). Anonymous committee members reportedly were embarrassed by how Kramer handled it and threatened to resign; critics also alleged the censure vote violated the committee's own procedural rules (not on the agenda, guests present during voting). I found no on-the-record rebuttal from Kramer addressing the procedural criticism specifically (he is only quoted on the "what does censure mean" exchange itself). Source: Vero Beach 32963, "Backlash Against Jay Kramer Over GOP Unit's Vote To Censure Barefoot," Sept. 23, 2021, https://www.vb32963online.com/STORIES%202021/SEPTEMBER%202021/VB32963_Backlash_Against_Jay_Kramer_Over_GOP_Units_Vote_To_Censure_Barefoot_Issue38_092321.html.
- A related item, "GOP Executive Committee here embroiled in turmoil" (Vero News, Oct. 28, 2021, http://veronews.com/2021/10/28/gop-executive-committee-here-embroiled-in-turmoil/), appears to cover the same or a related REC internal dispute; I did not fetch its full text given time constraints but flag it for anyone doing a deeper follow-up on his REC tenure specifically.

### 2016 County Commission District 5 run (post-council-service context)
- **Type: fact.** While serving as Mayor, Kramer filed (May 6, 2015) to challenge incumbent Commissioner Bob Solari in the 2016 Republican primary for County Commission District 5, running explicitly on his continued opposition to further electric-utility entanglements and general fiscal-conservative positioning; he had also served as the county's Republican State Committeeman, 2012–2016 (winning that committeeman post itself with 67.61% in a separate committee-only vote). Source: county VoterFocus candidate record, https://www.voterfocus.com/CampaignFinance/candidate_pr.php?op=cv&e=13&c=indian&ca=312&rellevel=4&committee=N.
- **Type: fact, moderately confident but not from a directly-read vote tally.** Kramer did not advance to the general election for that seat — the Nov. 8, 2016 general election in County Commission District 5 was contested between Bob Solari (R, 74.10%, 51,091 votes) and Brian Heady (running NPA, 25.90%, 17,862 votes), confirming Solari was the Republican nominee, which means Kramer lost the August 2016 Republican primary to Solari. I could not locate a directly-read primary vote-tally page (Florida's/Indian River County's 2016 primary-night results archive did not return readable content in this session), so **the primary loss is inferred from the general-election lineup rather than confirmed from a primary-night tally** — flagged as needing a follow-up direct read of the county's Aug. 30, 2016 primary results before stating an exact percentage. Source for the general-election tally: Florida Division of Elections Election Night Reporting, Indian River County, https://enr.electionsfl.org/IND/1638/Summary/ (fetched Aug. 26, 2026).

## BRIAN HEADY — Vero Beach City Council (one term, 2009–2011)

### The guide's gap: he DID serve, not just run
- **Type: fact. This corrects the prior research pass, which characterized him only as a "perennial candidate."** Heady was elected to Vero Beach City Council in 2009 — his one successful campaign among what is now reported as 26 total campaigns for office — riding a wave of voter anger over high Vero Electric bills (the same underlying utility issue that shaped Kramer's tenure; the two overlapped on the same council, with Kramer becoming Mayor partway through Heady's term). He served a full two-year term, 2009–2011, and lost his re-election bid at the end of it. Sources: Vero News, "Vero City Council race reminiscent of 2009," Sept. 9, 2021, http://veronews.com/2021/09/09/vero-city-council-race-reminiscent-of-2009/; Vero News, "Brian Heady sues Vero, alleging his rights have been violated," June 21, 2018, https://veronews.com/2018/06/21/brian-heady-sues-vero-alleging-rights-violated/ (retrospective bio paragraph); Vero Beach 32963, "Heady: Man of principle or professional contrarian?," Nov. 25, 2010, https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202010/NOVEMBER/vb32963_Heady_Issue36_112510.html.

### His record while in office
- **Type: vote, with a self-reversal.** In Nov. 2010, Heady cast a "no" vote on a motion related to selling Vero Electric to FPL, despite having spent months pushing for the city to at least discuss a sale with FPL. When asked about the apparent reversal he denied backtracking: "I did not backtrack. I have not changed my position," arguing the amended motion required proof the sale would be financially beneficial before he'd support it, and that he needed more information. Source: Vero Beach 32963, Nov. 25, 2010, cited above.
- **Type: fact (procedural/behavioral, not a formal vote).** On the same underlying issue, Heady opposed a colleague's (Councilman Craig Fletcher's) push for public meetings with FPL, instead advocating for private, one-on-one closed-door sessions between each council member and FPL — a position in some tension with his general "insist on full transparency" reputation (see below). The same article describes him placing 12 agenda items without the backup documentation the city required, during a nearly-5-hour meeting, while other members' items were properly documented; it also reports that Mayor Jay Kramer's strategy for handling Heady in meetings was to let him "talk himself out" rather than engage directly. Source: Vero Beach 32963, Nov. 25, 2010, cited above.
- **Type: fact — favorable characterization from a different outlet.** A more sympathetic retrospective describes Heady, during his term, as someone who "worked hard and was always accessible to the public," understood municipal issues, "voted his conscience, even if it meant playing the contrarian," and "insisted on full transparency at all times." Source: Vero News, "Vero City Council race reminiscent of 2009," Sept. 9, 2021, cited above. (Noted as a genuinely mixed record across sources — one outlet's "contrarian/undocumented agenda items" framing and another's "principled/accessible" framing are not contradictory as much as differently emphasized; both are reported here rather than picking one.)
- **Type: fact/allegation, in which he is the plaintiff, not a subject of allegation against him.** While still sitting on the council, Heady sued the city in federal court in May 2010, alleging officials failed to act transparently in negotiating a 2007 contract with the Orlando Utilities Commission — specifically that the city refused public access to strategy sessions and contract documents related to the electric utility. Source: Vero News, June 21, 2018 (retrospective), cited above.

### Post-council lawsuits and reputation as a public commenter
- **Type: fact/allegation (his allegations against the city), with no clear on-record city rebuttal found.** In a separate June 2018 lawsuit, Heady alleged the City Council "violates my rights at almost every city council meeting," that officials had him removed from council chambers by police "to hide from public view frauds on the public," and brought three counts: Denial of Rights, Unjust Enrichment, and Public Ridicule and Embarrassment. The article reports that "all the lawsuits were eventually dismissed" in reference to his cumulative legal history against the city dating to 2010, but does not give the specific 2018 case's individual outcome. Source: Vero News, "Brian Heady sues Vero, alleging his rights have been violated," June 21, 2018, https://veronews.com/2018/06/21/brian-heady-sues-vero-alleging-rights-violated/.
- **Type: fact.** He self-published a book, titled (per the article) "Liars, Cheats, and Thieves," under a pen name, described as documenting his view of "injustices and corruption" in city government. Same source.
- **Type: fact.** In the Nov. 2016 general election, running with no party affiliation, Heady challenged Bob Solari for County Commission District 5 (a *county*, not city, race) and lost, 25.90% to 74.10% (17,862 to 51,091 votes). This is separate from his city council history but corroborates the "prolific candidate for many different offices" characterization and independently establishes that his NPA filing in that race is what forced a closed (Republican-only) primary between Solari and Kramer that year, rather than the open "universal primary" that would otherwise have applied. Source: Florida Division of Elections Election Night Reporting, https://enr.electionsfl.org/IND/1638/Summary/; contextual detail on the "closes the primary" effect via Indian River Guardian, "Heady shuts 55,000 voters out of County Commission District 5 primary election," June 30, 2016, https://indianriverguardian.com/2016/06/30/heady-shuts-55000-voters-out-of-county-commission-district-5-primary-election/ (an opinion-leaning outlet critical of Heady on this point — treat the "shuts out" framing as their editorial characterization of an otherwise-factual procedural mechanism).

### 2026 candidacy — confirmed
- **Type: fact.** Heady is one of the eight officially qualified 2026 candidates. Source: City of Vero Beach official candidate list, https://www.covb.org/659/Candidates-For-City-Council-2026 (fetched Aug. 26, 2026), filing email brianheady65@gmail.com.

### What this means for the guide's current entry
The current guide text — "a perennial Vero Beach candidate reported to be running his 26th campaign for office... His past stated focus has been opposing what he calls wasteful council spending on studies" — is not wrong, but it is incomplete in a way that matters: it implies (without saying) that he has never held the office he's now seeking. **He has, for one term, 18 years ago**, and that term is well-documented, including a vote-reversal and an in-office lawsuit against the same city government he's again asking voters to let him help govern. This is directly comparable in kind (though not volume) to the sitting incumbents' documented records, and the guide should say so rather than lump him in with truly first-time candidates.

## TRACEY ZUDANS — additional record beyond the existing 2024 recusal entry

The guide already documents her 2024 recusal (sourced to a partisan Substack). Below is additional material from her single term (elected Nov. 2022, served through Nov. 2024) found in a reasonably thorough (not exhaustive) pass, per the task's equal-depth instruction now that Kramer and Heady are getting fuller treatment.

### FY2023-24 budget/millage vote — lone dissent (new item, not currently in the guide)
- **Type: vote.** Zudans cast the sole dissenting vote on both the millage-rate increase and the FY2023-24 budget, on consecutive 4-1 votes in Sept. 2023 (effective Oct. 1, 2023). The $33 million budget (up 5.1%) raised the millage from $2.69 to $2.77 per $1,000 of taxable value, funding four new police officers, added planning/finance staff, and 5% raises for 330+ municipal employees, generating about $1.55 million in added tax revenue. Zudans called the budget's spending "unnecessary" and argued the council could have "met the public-safety criteria without raising the millage," noting the millage had already risen in back-to-back years. Mayor Cotugno defended the increase as "reasonable" given growth and public-safety needs. Source: Vero News, "City ups taxes to fund raises, add cops," Oct. 30, 2023, https://veronews.com/2023/10/30/city-ups-taxes-to-fund-raises-add-cops/.

### Three Corners / SuDa saga — fuller detail on the recusal already in the guide
The guide currently has one vague, partisan-sourced item dated 2024-01-01 about a recusal tied to a "politically threatening" email. Direct research surfaces a fuller, more precise picture:
- **Type: vote.** On May 28, 2024, Zudans voted with the council majority (3-2, per the guide's existing Moore-record item) to pursue the "SuDa" Three Corners redevelopment proposal over "Clearpath."
- **Type: fact, with named source of the pressure.** Following that vote, Brian Barefoot (the same former School Board Chairman whom Jay Kramer's REC later censured — an unrelated coincidence of the same recurring local political figure) sent Zudans an email suggesting his support for her concurrent County Commission District 5 campaign was contingent on her SuDa vote. She characterized it as: "support for my run for county commission was based on my vote, or not on my vote."
- **Type: vote/fact.** At a June 7, 2024 special meeting, the council voted 3-1 (one abstention) to disqualify SuDa's proposal, and separately 4-1 to restart the developer-selection process entirely. Zudans recused herself specifically from the disqualification vote, citing the Barefoot email — even though the city attorney had advised her that the email did not meet Florida's statutory recusal requirements. She recused anyway.
- Source for this fuller detail: The Sunshine Journal (Thomas Augustus's Substack — the same partisan source already cited in the guide's existing Zudans entry; this guide continues to treat it as opinion-leaning but is using it here only for the factual sequence of votes/dates/quotes, which is internally consistent and specific enough to be usable): https://thomasaugustus.substack.com/p/update-mayor-cotugnos-political-failure. **I did not find an independent, non-Substack confirmation of the exact June 7, 2024 vote tallies** (a direct read of that date's council minutes on covb.org would resolve this) — flagged as a follow-up rather than treated as fully independently corroborated.

### Conflict-of-interest question while dual-tracking her county campaign (Dec. 2023)
- **Type: fact/allegation (implicit — raised by the situation, not a formal charge), with her direct response.** While running for County Commission District 5, Zudans continued serving on city council and was asked about potential conflicts, particularly around Indian River Shores' pending water-sewer franchise renewal with Vero Beach (relevant to funding the city's wastewater plant relocation). Her response: "I can't even think of an issue where there would be a conflict," while affirming she saw her "fiduciary duty to Vero Beach" as her "first responsibility." Mayor Cotugno said "potential exists" for conflicts; her county-race opponent Laura Moss and former Commissioner Bob Solari both said they didn't see it as compromising. No specific instance of an actual conflicted vote was reported in this article or found elsewhere in this pass. Source: Vero News, "Zudans, seeking County Commission seat, sees no conflict with city duties," Dec. 1, 2023, https://veronews.com/2023/12/01/zudans-seeking-county-commission-seat-sees-no-conflict-with-city-duties/.

### Term boundaries
- **Type: fact.** Zudans was elected Nov. 2022 (leading the field with 15.49%, 2,434 votes) and her term ran through Nov. 2024, when Aaron Vos was sworn in to replace her — she did not seek re-election to the council seat, running instead (unsuccessfully) for County Commission. Source: Yahoo News/hometownnewstc aggregation, "Vero Beach City Council: Voters reject two incumbents; Carroll, Moore, Zudans elected," https://news.yahoo.com/vero-beach-city-council-voters-032306575.html; Vero Beach 32963, "Vos, Elected To Vero Council, Vows To Do What's Best For Our City," Nov. 14, 2024, https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202024/NOVEMBER%202024/VB32963_Vos_Elected_To_Vero_Council_Vows_To_Do_Whats_Best_For_Our_City_Issue46_111424.html.

### One caution flagged, not adopted
A search result surfaced an unrelated 2019 controversy (River House brewery proposal, Leisure Square pool closure attempt, skate park, Marina outside-management proposal, Dodgertown golf course sale attempt) that a secondary source loosely associated with "Zudans." **On closer reading this appears to concern Val Zudans (Tracey's husband, a former Vero Beach mayor and, separately, a state legislator), not Tracey Zudans, who was not yet on the council in 2019.** I did not include this in her record and flag it explicitly so it isn't mistakenly attributed to her in a later pass — this is exactly the kind of same-surname mixup the guide should guard against.
