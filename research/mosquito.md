# PART 1 — WHAT THIS DISTRICT DOES AND WHAT IT COSTS YOU

## 1.1 It is a separate government you already pay taxes to

**[VERIFIED FACT]** The Indian River Mosquito Control District (IRMCD) is an **independent special district** — a unit of local government legally separate from Indian River County, with its own elected board and its own property-tax levy. It was **established by the Legislature in 1925** and **re-created by special act under Chapter 2006-344, Laws of Florida**. In 2011, Chapter 2011-243, Laws of Florida, revised the board's powers to authorize it to do what is necessary "for the control of mosquitoes and sandflies and diseases transmitted by the same" in the district.
*Source: OPPAGA / The Balmoral Group, "Indian River Mosquito Control District Review — Final Report," September 2023, p.4. https://oppaga.fl.gov/Documents/ContractedReviews/Indian%20River%20MCD%20Final%20Report.pdf*

**[VERIFIED FACT]** It operates under: Ch. 189 F.S. (independent special districts), Ch. 388 F.S. (mosquito control districts), and Ch. 5E-13 Florida Administrative Code (Dept. of Agriculture & Consumer Services rules for mosquito control programs).
*Source: same OPPAGA report, p.4.*

**[VERIFIED FACT]** Governance: **three commissioners, elected countywide to four-year terms.** Board officers include a chair, vice chair, and secretary/treasurer. A commissioner must be a qualified elector of the district. The board is required by s.388.151 F.S. to meet at least monthly; meetings are public and noticed under s.189.015 F.S., with minutes and agendas published online.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.4.*

**Why this matters for the voter:** three people control a multi-million-dollar tax-funded agency, and each is elected by the whole county. With only three seats, **a single seat is one-third of the entire governing body.**

## 1.2 What it costs you — taxing authority, millage, budget

**[VERIFIED FACT]** IRMCD levies its own ad valorem (property) tax. The board sets the millage rate each year. The county Property Appraiser certifies the roll and the county Tax Collector collects the money on the district's behalf. Fiscal year runs Oct 1 – Sep 30.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.9; district budget page https://www.irmosquito.com/budget*

**[VERIFIED FACT] — Millage history (most recent independently documented figures):**

| Fiscal Year | Millage rate | Total taxable value |
|---|---|---|
| FY 2019-20 | 0.2515 | $18.567 billion |
| FY 2020-21 | 0.2515 | $19.559 billion |
| FY 2021-22 | 0.2515 | $20.577 billion |
| FY 2022-23 | 0.2500 | $23.357 billion |

*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, Table 1, p.3 (data from Florida Dept. of Revenue).*

**Plain English:** a millage of 0.25 = **25 cents per $1,000 of taxable value**. On a home with $300,000 of taxable value, that is roughly **$75 a year**. On $200,000 taxable value, about **$50 a year**. It is a small line item on your TRIM notice — but it is a real, separate tax, voted on by three people you elect.

## 1.3 What it actually does, operationally

**[VERIFIED FACT]** The district serves **352 square miles** — the eastern portion of Indian River County — from headquarters at 5655 41st Street, Vero Beach. It had **35 paid staff**, 4 boats, 30 trucks and vans, 15 utility vehicles, one facility with 7 buildings (FY 2021-22). **50 mosquito species** are known in the county.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.1-2, 5, Table 5.*

**[VERIFIED FACT]** It runs all eight areas of Integrated Pest Management:

- **Mosquito surveillance** — ground and aerial surveillance; CDC light traps, landing-rate counts; traps baited with CO2, octanol, or light. **Trap collections happen three days a week** — more frequent than peer districts such as Citrus and South Walton County MCDs, which collect weekly.
- **Disease surveillance** — participating **since 1978** in the state arbovirus surveillance program for St. Louis encephalitis (SLE), West Nile virus (WNV), and Eastern Equine encephalitis (EEE). Uses **48 sentinel chickens across 8 coop sites**; blood samples go weekly to a Florida Dept. of Health laboratory. Also intensively monitors the key vector mosquito *Culex nigripalpus* at two locations. Dengue, chikungunya, and Zika have **no early-warning chicken test** — they pass mosquito→human only — so the only signal is a reported human case, tracked via DOH weekly reports.
- **Source reduction** — "tip and toss" container work; **Rotational Impoundment Management (RIM)**: roughly **2,600 acres of saltmarsh wetlands along the Indian River Lagoon are impounded and managed**, flooded seasonally with pumps so saltmarsh mosquitoes cannot lay eggs — which **eliminates the need for chemical treatment in those areas.** Also a **waste-tire program running since 1992**: over **1,127 tons of tires** collected and disposed of in 30 years (11.3 tons FY19-20, 15.18 tons FY20-21, 3.68 tons FY21-22).
- **Larviciding** — focused on saltmarsh wetlands along the Lagoon; oils and films, soil bacterium (Bti-type), and insect growth regulators, applied by truck or ATV.
- **Adulticiding** — **truck-based Ultra-Low Volume (ULV) spraying after sunset**, decided day-to-day based on surveillance and state criteria.
- **Aerial adulticiding** — the district does **not** routinely spray from the air. It **contracts with an outside aerial-spray company to apply Dibrom (naled, an organophosphate insecticide) only during widespread outbreaks or a medical emergency** involving SLE, West Nile, or Zika. **[VERIFIED FACT] There were no such outbreaks in the four fiscal years OPPAGA reviewed, so no aerial-spray contract costs were incurred.**
- **Biological / alternative control** — a **mosquito-eating fish (Gambusia) hatchery**.
- **Research** — collaborations with the University of Florida's Florida Medical Entomology Laboratory (FMEL) in Vero Beach, USFWS, FDEP, Harbor Branch Oceanographic Institution, Florida A&M; regular efficacy testing of its own pesticides.
- **Outreach and education** — schools, HOAs, civic and church groups, community events.

*Source for all of the above: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.6-8 and Table 6.*

**[VERIFIED FACT]** The district coordinates with St. Johns River Water Management District, FDACS, FDEP, state parks and aquatic preserves, FWC, USFWS, and the Florida Dept. of Health, and provides service in/near Fellsmere, Vero Beach, Sebastian, and Indian River Shores.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.5.*

**[VERIFIED FACT]** The district reported receiving **over 1,000 service calls in each of the past three fiscal years** (as of the 2023 review), with a stated objective of responding within two business days.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.18-19, Table 15.*

## 1.4 Disease context

**[VERIFIED FACT]** **There were no locally acquired arbovirus cases in humans in Indian River County during the calendar year of the review and the three previous calendar years.** The only case detected in the district was **one travel-related case of dengue fever.** OPPAGA credited "effective monitoring and treatments."
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.19 (and Executive Summary, p.i).*

**Voter framing:** the district's central performance claim is that it holds local mosquito-borne disease at zero. That is also the argument for why its budget is hard to cut — the visible result of the money is an absence of something. Diseases it screens for: St. Louis encephalitis, West Nile virus, Eastern Equine encephalitis; it also tracks dengue, chikungunya, and Zika through human case reports.

## 1.5 Environmental / pesticide / spending controversy — what the record shows

**[VERIFIED FACT] — No aerial spraying in the review period.** As above: aerial application of Dibrom (naled) is reserved for outbreaks/medical emergencies and **was not triggered in the four fiscal years reviewed.** This directly bears on the pollinator-and-aerial-spray concern that drives mosquito-control controversy elsewhere in Florida. In Indian River, the routine adulticiding is truck-mounted ULV applied after sunset (when honeybees are not foraging).
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.7 and p.13-14 area (contracted services).*

**[VERIFIED FACT] — Lagoon impact is framed by the district and its reviewer as a positive.** The Rotational Impoundment Management program on 2,600 acres of Indian River Lagoon saltmarsh is described by OPPAGA as "a unique example of effective mosquito control in vast coastal, environmentally sensitive areas" that "serve[s] as a model for nearby coastal counties" (Brevard, St. Lucie, Martin), and it **removes the need for chemical treatment in impounded areas.** IRMCD is described as "the only district responsible for managing mosquito impoundments" of that Lagoon saltmarsh.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.2 and p.15-16.*

**[VERIFIED FACT] — Audit findings, both corrected.** The FY 2018-19 audit carried forward an uncorrected FY 2017-18 issue: an amount recorded for chemical inventory did not align with the annual inventory report. It was corrected by FY 2019-20. The FY 2019-20 audit raised a new finding about a **vendor's chemical donation** being misfiled in inventory/capital assets; that was corrected in the FY 2020-21 audit, which found nothing further. Overall: **"no material findings nor weakness in internal controls."**
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.15.*

**[VERIFIED FACT] — A consolidation question exists, raised by the County.** The **Chair of the Indian River Board of County Commissioners told OPPAGA's reviewer that the district "seems to run efficiently"** but that **consolidating the district into county government should be considered to improve accountability and transparency,** and that moving it under the county "would improve cost effectiveness and transparency to citizens." **OPPAGA's reviewer disagreed and did not recommend consolidation**, finding limited efficiency gains available because mosquito-control operations are specialized.
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, p.8-9.*
**This is the single most useful live policy question in this race** — the only documented, on-the-record criticism of the district's existence, and it came from the chair of the county commission, not an activist.

**[VERIFIED FACT] — OPPAGA's actual criticisms of the district:**
1. The district "lacks measurable performance standards with which to assess how well it meets its newly-defined goals and objectives."
2. It "has not yet actively managed performance success or failure of its operations and administration against goals, and does not currently have effective reporting mechanisms in place to measure results on a timely basis."
3. Its strategic plan was too new to show progress.
4. It reported over 1,000 service calls a year but **did not report how many of those calls it actually responded to.**
Recommendation: formalize additional performance measures/standards. A second recommendation went to the Legislature (amend s.388.46 F.S. to have the Florida Coordinating Council on Mosquito Control develop model standards statewide).
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, Executive Summary p.i, p.15, p.19-21.*

**[VERIFIED FACT]** The district's own strategic-plan goals include "Achieve Sustainable Funding Sources," with objectives to post draft and final budgets on the website, **"Develop reserve fund balance policy,"** develop a 5-year Capital Improvement Plan, and create a review process for alternative funding sources. OPPAGA noted the district's focus has been to **build its reserve for capital improvements.**
*Source: OPPAGA report Sept 2023, Table 15 p.17 and p.16.*
**Voter framing:** "the district is accumulating reserves while revenue exceeds spending" is a legitimate, document-backed line of questioning — revenues exceeded expenditures in FY 2020-21 and FY 2021-22.

**[NOT FOUND / NO EVIDENCE]** No documented controversy specific to IRMCD over pollinator kills, bee die-offs, aerial-spray litigation, or environmental damage to the Indian River Lagoon was located in this research. **Stating that plainly is the honest finding.** The general Florida controversy over naled/Dibrom aerial spraying exists, but IRMCD's record in the reviewed period shows it did not aerially spray.
