## LINDA MOORE — Vero Beach City Council (currently miscategorized as "Vice Mayor" in guide.js)

### Title correction
- **Current title: Councilmember** (not Vice Mayor). Source: City of Vero Beach official council roster, https://www.covb.org/283/City-Council (fetched 2026-08-26).
- She served as Vice Mayor from Nov. 2023 through Nov. 17, 2025 (elected 3-2 in Nov. 2023 and again in Nov. 2024). At the Nov. 17, 2025 organizational meeting, she nominated Councilman Taylor Dingle for Vice Mayor instead of seeking another term herself; Dingle won 3-2 (Cotugno/Moore/Dingle vs. Carroll/Vos who both backed Carroll). John Cotugno was simultaneously re-elected Mayor, 3-2 (same lineup). Source: Vero Beach 32963, "John Cotugno Re-Elected As Vero Mayor," week of Nov. 20, 2025, https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202025/NOVEMBER%202025/VB32963_John_Cotugno_Re-Elected_As_Vero_Mayor_Issue47_112025.html; corroborated by city council organizational meeting minutes, https://www.covb.org/DocumentCenter/View/8887/2A-2 (11/17/25).

### FY2025-26 budget vote
- Vice-Mayor Moore made the motion to approve the city's FY2025-26 millage rate; Councilman Vos seconded it. The council voted unanimously (5-0) on Sept. 4, 2025 for a $36 million budget funded by a 2.9816 millage rate — 7.72% higher than the prior year, raising an additional ~$1.7 million, cited as needed for seven new employee positions, personnel cost increases, and a 2% raise for retirees. Source: Hometown News, "Vero Beach approves $36m city budget with higher tax rate," https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/vero-beach-approves-36m-city-budget-with-higher-tax-rate/article_e73e323f-e8a2-5b1e-b911-2e7922c34448.html (dated to the Sept. 4, 2025 meeting; exact bylline date not independently re-confirmed after a rate-limited refetch, but content is consistent across search excerpts).

### Three Corners redevelopment project (former power-plant waterfront site)
- **She initially opposed the eventual winning developer.** In an earlier-term council vote (referenced retrospectively), Moore voted with then-Councilman Taylor Dingle and then-Councilwoman Tracey Zudans to pursue negotiations with the rival "SuDa" proposal over the "Clearpath" proposal — a 3-2 vote. That SuDa group was later disqualified by the council (a separate, contested 3-1 vote in June 2024 in which Zudans recused herself; already documented in the guide under Zudans' entry). Source: Vero News, "Three Corners: Council seen backing Clearpath," by Ray McNulty, April 11, 2025, https://veronews.com/2025/04/11/three-corners-council-seen-backing-clearpath/.
- **Final vote:** On April 22, 2025, the council voted (reported as unanimous, 5-0, across multiple news accounts) to select Clearpath as the Three Corners developer. Moore was present and spoke before the vote; TCPalm's photo caption identifies her in that context, but I could not independently confirm from a readable-text source whether she personally voted yes or was recorded as a dissent — the "unanimous" characterization comes from aggregated search summaries of Yahoo/TCPalm coverage (Nick Slater, TCPalm/Treasure Coast Newspapers, published April 24, 2025) and other outlets, not a directly-read vote tally. **Flagging this as needing a follow-up direct read of the April 22, 2025 minutes (https://www.covb.org/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_04222025-1797) before stating a specific tally as fact** — that PDF would not render as text via automated fetch in this research session.
- **Three Corners lease-extension vote, Aug. 2026:** Moore voted for (or at least did not block) a second 30-day extension on ground-lease negotiations, but signaled she would not support a third: "I'm okay with this extension, but I don't think I'd be okay with another one. It feels like we're stalling a little bit." Source: WQCS, "Three Corners project gets additional 30 day extension in Vero Beach," by Justin Breckenridge, Aug. 13, 2026, https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2026-08-13/three-corners-project-gets-additional-30-day-extension-in-vero-beach.

### Old nursery site / affordable housing (Live Local Act) — Dec. 2025
- **Moore was on the losing side of a divided council vote.** She and Mayor Cotugno voted to use a 14-acre city-owned "old nursery site" (along the FEC railway tracks, adjacent to Crestlawn Cemetery) for affordable housing under the state's Live Local Act. Councilman Vos, Vice Mayor Dingle, and Councilman Carroll voted against, and the proposal failed. Moore said: "This is making me crazy, that we're not going to do affordable housing because of hurricane debris." She was also quoted elsewhere arguing a citywide affordable-housing referendum "will fail because of the NIMBYs (Not In My Back Yard) in our community that don't want anything like this." Source: Vero News, "On housing site, Vero's tough call was right one," by Ray McNulty, Dec. 30, 2025, https://veronews.com/2025/12/30/on-housing-site-veros-tough-call-was-right-one/.

### Live Local Act zoning update / "Yes in God's Backyard" — Jan. 2026
- The council voted unanimously to direct staff to incorporate new state Senate Bill 1730 provisions (which let religious institutions build workforce housing on their property, with a 10% affordable-unit set-aside) into an ongoing rewrite of the city's single-family zoning code. Moore raised a concern about the provision creating potential religious discrimination, saying she didn't want the city to "open that can of worms" regarding whether such housing could be restricted to members of a specific faith, while stating she remained broadly supportive of measures making affordable housing easier to build. She still voted for the directive along with the rest of the council. Source: Hometown News, "Vero Beach updates zoning for Live Local rules," by Donald Rodrigue, published Jan. 27, 2026 (updated Feb. 3, 2026), https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/vero-beach-updates-zoning-for-live-local-rules/article_70137709-1754-55c1-a42d-0c8edfe1d7a9.html.

### Public conduct amid a 2025 controversy involving her privately-owned business
Per the task's instructions, I am reporting only the portion of this that is a matter of public record involving her conduct as an officeholder — not her private business's programming or her personal social media activity, which I am excluding.
- In summer 2025, a controversy arose over a drag-themed event held at Moore's privately-owned restaurant (the Kilted Mermaid). This is **not** a city event, ordinance, or council action — it was private business programming. It became a subject of public comment at a Vero Beach City Council meeting: of 24 speakers on the topic, 17 spoke in support of Moore. Facing public calls for her resignation, Moore — acting in her public capacity as the sitting Vice Mayor — stated she would not resign. Separately, Jennifer Pippin, chair of the local Moms for Liberty chapter, filed a complaint with the Florida Attorney General's office alleging the event exposed children to a "sexualized performance." I found no indication of any city council vote, ordinance, or official proclamation connected to this controversy — it did not generate city policy action; it generated public comment and Moore's public statement about her own tenure. Source: Vero News, "Support for vice mayor, tolerance run deep in city," by Ray McNulty, July 31, 2025, https://veronews.com/2025/07/31/support-for-vice-mayor-tolerance-run-deep-in-city/.
- I deliberately excluded: the content/details of the private event itself, an unrelated April 2024 personal Facebook post of hers that generated separate criticism (a personal social-media matter, not an official act), and any Facebook-page or social-media-only commentary/endorsement material (e.g., a since-found Indian River Democratic Party Facebook video referencing her) — all per the "no private life / no social media as if public record" instruction.
- **Equal-depth check:** I searched for a comparable public controversy or resignation-pressure episode involving any of the other seven candidates in this race and found none. This is reported as a genuine asymmetry, not manufactured balance.

### Campaign finance / endorsements, 2026 cycle

The City of Vero Beach's DocumentCenter has her 2022- and 2024-cycle reports indexed (e.g., G1–G8, M6–M9 filings) but I found no 2026-cycle ("P"-prefixed, matching the naming convention used for opponent Tracey Zudans' "P3" 2026 report at https://www.covb.org/DocumentCenter/View/9250/) report for Moore as of this research date. ## AARON VOS — Vero Beach City Council (incumbent since Nov. 2024)

### FY2025-26 budget vote
- Seconded Vice-Mayor Moore's motion to approve the FY2025-26 millage rate (2.9816 mills, a 7.72% increase); final vote was unanimous, 5-0, Sept. 4, 2025. During budget deliberations he separately advocated for using AI to assist with crime-data analysis as part of the police budget discussion. Source: Hometown News, "Vero Beach approves $36m city budget with higher tax rate," https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/vero-beach-approves-36m-city-budget-with-higher-tax-rate/article_e73e323f-e8a2-5b1e-b911-2e7922c34448.html.

### Three Corners redevelopment
- As a newly-seated council member in April 2025 (having replaced Zudans), Vos was expected by press coverage to defer to the developer-selection committee's recommendation of Clearpath rather than revisit the prior council's rejected SuDa pick, and the April 22, 2025 vote for Clearpath is reported across multiple outlets as unanimous. Source: Vero News, "Three Corners: Council seen backing Clearpath," April 11, 2025, https://veronews.com/2025/04/11/three-corners-council-seen-backing-clearpath/. (Same caveat as above: I have not independently read a plain-text vote tally from the April 22 minutes.)
- **Three Corners lease-extension vote, Aug. 2026:** Vos supported granting a second 30-day extension on ground-lease negotiations but pushed for tighter oversight going forward: "Probably a little more rigor on the next round. Maybe have more of a briefing or an update that helps qualify another extension." Source: WQCS, Aug. 13, 2026, https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2026-08-13/three-corners-project-gets-additional-30-day-extension-in-vero-beach.

### Old nursery site / affordable housing — Dec. 2025 (opposed Moore)
- Vos voted against using the 14-acre "old nursery site" for affordable housing (joined by Vice Mayor Dingle and Councilman Carroll; opposed by Moore and Mayor Cotugno). He argued the site should be preserved as a "required city resource," drawing an analogy: "This site is a required city resource. It's just like a fire truck. We might not need a fire truck every day, but when you need it, you need it." He also raised environmental/health concerns tied to the site's industrial zoning and a nearby cement operation. Source: Vero News, "On housing site, Vero's tough call was right one," Dec. 30, 2025, https://veronews.com/2025/12/30/on-housing-site-veros-tough-call-was-right-one/.

### City financial-reporting failures / state enforcement action — 2025
- Notable watchdog role: following a February 2025 state enforcement action against the city (tied to late state sales-tax payments and two years of delayed pension contributions, which cost the city over $250,000 in penalties/interest), Vos conducted his own independent "fishbone diagram" root-cause analysis of the failures, telling colleagues: "There are more yellows, which means potential contributor to the issue, than I've ever seen" in comparable corrective-action studies. He pushed for a shared calendar of financial filing deadlines and gave "full-throated support" to Councilman Dingle's motion demanding the city resume monthly financial reports (Mayor Cotugno had proposed delaying that resumption until August). Vice Mayor Moore separately criticized the prior practice of printed quarterly reports as "madness" and favored simpler digital reporting. Source: Vero Beach 32963, "Council Demands Monthly Reports On City Finances," by Lisa Zahner, week of June 5, 2025, https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202025/JUNE%202025/VB32963_Council_Demands_Monthly_Reports_On_City_Finances_Issue23_060525.html.

### Nov. 2025 organizational meeting
- Vos nominated Councilman John Carroll for both Mayor and Vice Mayor; both nominations lost 3-2 to Cotugno (Mayor) and Dingle (Vice Mayor). Source: covb.org organizational meeting minutes, 11/17/25, https://www.covb.org/DocumentCenter/View/8887/2A-2; corroborated by Vero Beach 32963, Nov. 20, 2025 issue, cited above.

### Campaign finance / endorsements, 2026 cycle

## TERTIARY SPOT CHECKS

### Deryl Loar (County Commission D4) — one new dated update
- The guide's existing entry (as of this research) describes the sheriff-vs-commission budget lawsuit as unresolved ("What happens from FY2027-28 onward is unresolved"). **That has since been resolved by settlement.** Terms: the county will provide an additional $3.5 million to the Sheriff's Office for FY2025-26 and commit to a $4 million increase for the Sheriff's FY2026-27 budget; in exchange, Sheriff Eric Flowers agreed to dismiss both the pending lawsuit and the administrative appeal, and not to pursue further mid-year budget amendments except in emergencies. As Board Chairman, Loar said: "The Board affirms its commitment to putting recent conflicts behind and moving forward in a cooperative, professional partnership with the Sheriff's Office for the benefit of all residents." Source: WPTV, "Indian River County settles budget dispute with sheriff, provides $3.5 million," by Scott Sutton, https://www.wptv.com/news/treasure-coast/region-indian-river-county/indian-river-county-settles-budget-dispute-with-sheriff-provides-3-5-million (dated on/around April 14, 2026 per photo caption). **This should be added to Loar's record and the "unresolved" framing in the race's `stakes` text should be updated**, since the underlying premise of that stakes paragraph (the dispute's outcome being unknown) is now out of date.
- I did not find a specific commission vote tally on the settlement itself in this pass; that would need a direct read of the relevant commission minutes if the guide wants to cite a tally rather than just the outcome.

### Kerry Ann Bartlett (Hospital District Seat 6, unopposed) — sourced her existing bio claim + one new item

I located and confirmed the quote and can now source it: "I think we have a responsibility to be strategic with our funding. I don't know about the word frugal." This appears to be from an earlier point in the FY25-26 budget cycle.
- **New, more specific item, Aug. 4, 2026:** The board approved the FY2025-26 maximum millage rate of 74.5 cents per $1,000 of assessed value (above the 71.2-cent rollback rate) on a 5-1 vote, with Trustee Paul Westcott dissenting — a $1.2 million tax increase over the prior year, on a total budget of $25.37 million (roughly double the FY2021-22 budget). Bartlett's "strategic... not frugal" quote is attributed to her in this same context as board chair. Source: Vero News, "Hospital District can't seem to curb its spending habits," by Lisa Zahner, Aug. 4, 2026, https://veronews.com/2026/08/04/hospital-district-cant-seem-to-curb-its-spending-habits/.
