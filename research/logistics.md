## 5. FLORIDA'S CLOSED PRIMARY — WHAT NPA VOTERS CAN ACTUALLY DO ON AUG 18

**CONFIRMED.** The task brief's framing is correct. Source (verbatim quotes below):
https://www.voteindianriver.gov/election_information/florida_is_a_closed_primary_state.php

### The general rule
> *"Florida is a closed primary election state. Only voters who are registered members of political parties may vote for respective party candidates or nominees for an office in a primary election."*

So: a registered Republican gets Republican partisan contests; a registered Democrat gets Democratic partisan contests; an NPA voter gets **neither party's partisan contests**.

### ⚠️ THE PART NPA VOTERS GET WRONG — YOU STILL HAVE A BALLOT ON AUG 18

The SOE page states two explicit exceptions where **all** registered voters vote, regardless of party or lack of one:

**Exception 1 — Nonpartisan races and local questions (verbatim):**
> *"If races for nonpartisan (i.e., free from party affiliation) judicial and school board offices, nonpartisan special districts or local referendum questions are on the primary election ballot, then all registered voters, **including those without party affiliation**, are entitled to vote those races on the ballot."*

In Indian River County in 2026 this covers: **School Board (Districts 1, 2, 4)**, **judicial races**, **Indian River County Hospital District (Seats 2, 4, 6)**, **Soil & Water Conservation District (Seats 1, 3, 5)**, **Mosquito Control District (Seats 2, 3)**, and any **local referendum questions**.
(Offices up for election source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/campaigns/candidates/offices_up_for_election.php)

**Exception 2 — Universal Primary Contest (verbatim):**
> *"If all the candidates for an office have the same party affiliation and the winner of the primary election will not face any opposition in the general election (i.e. no write-in candidates have qualified), then all registered voters can vote for any of the candidates for that office in the primary election."*

**Bottom line for NPA voters: DO show up on Aug 18.** You will not get partisan primary contests, but you will get a real ballot with School Board, special districts, judicial races, and any referendum questions — races that are frequently decided outright in August.

### General Election is different
> *"At a general election, all registered voters receive the same ballot and may vote for any candidate or question on the ballot."*
Party affiliation is irrelevant on Nov 3.

## 7. WRITE-IN EFFECT — COUNTY COMMISSION DISTRICT 2

### The rule (fully confirmed, official source)
From the SOE Closed Primary page, verbatim — note the parenthetical, which is the whole mechanism:
> *"If all the candidates for an office have the same party affiliation and the winner of the primary election will not face any opposition in the general election **(i.e. no write-in candidates have qualified)**, then all registered voters can vote for any of the candidates for that office in the primary election."*
Source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/election_information/florida_is_a_closed_primary_state.php

### What that means mechanically
A race where every candidate shares one party would normally become a **Universal Primary Contest (UPC)** — open to every registered voter, NPA included, because the August winner would automatically be the officeholder.

**A qualified write-in candidate destroys that.** Because the write-in's name-blank appears on the November General ballot, the primary winner *technically* faces November opposition. The UPC exception no longer applies, and the contest reverts to a **closed** partisan primary:

- **Aug 18 Primary:** ONLY voters registered in that candidates' party may vote the race. NPA voters and voters of other parties **do not see this contest** on their primary ballot.
- **Nov 3 General:** the race appears for **all** voters — the primary winner versus a blank write-in line. Verbatim: *"If there are write-in candidates who have qualified for a particular office, a space will be left on the ballot where their name can be written."* A write-in's name is **never printed**; the voter must physically write it in.

A write-in candidate does not have to campaign, pay a qualifying fee, or receive any votes for this effect to occur. Qualifying alone triggers it.

### 🛑 CORRECTION — the brief's premise does NOT hold for District 2

**Nicole Paradiso's write-in qualification is CONFIRMED.** Official county candidate record lists her as **"Nicole Paradiso (WRI) — Active-Qualified Write-In"** for *"Board of County Commissioners - Dist 2."*
Source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/campaigns/candidates/financial_reports_profiles.php (the SOE's official candidate profiles & financials page, serving county candidate-qualifying data)

**BUT the stated consequence is wrong.** The brief says her qualification "closes what would otherwise be a universal primary." It does not — **because District 2 was never going to be a universal primary in the first place.** The UPC exception requires that **all** candidates share one party. They do not.

**Certified District 2 field (same source):**

| Candidate | Party | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Robert (Robbie) D. Hardingham | **DEM** | Qualified |
| Bob McPartlan | REP | Qualified |
| Jennifer A. Pippin | REP | Qualified |
| Daniel Brooks Robinson | REP | Qualified |
| Nicole Paradiso | **WRI** | Qualified Write-In |
| ~~Melissa Dean~~ | REP | **Withdrawn** — not on ballot |
| ~~Damien H Gilliams~~ | REP | **Withdrawn** — not on ballot |

A **Democrat (Hardingham) qualified.** That alone guarantees November opposition and closes the Republican primary — with or without a write-in. Paradiso's candidacy is therefore **not** the thing keeping NPA voters out of this race; **Hardingham is.** Removing the write-in from the picture would change nothing about who votes Aug 18.

### So who actually votes in District 2, and when?

**Aug 18 Primary — a closed REPUBLICAN primary only:**
- **Registered Republicans** vote a contested three-way race: **McPartlan vs. Pippin vs. Robinson.**
- **Registered Democrats** — Hardingham is unopposed for the Democratic nomination, so there is no Democratic contest to vote in this race.
- **NPA and minor-party voters do NOT see County Commission District 2 on their Aug 18 ballot.** (They still get School Board, special districts, and any referendum questions — see §5.)

**Nov 3 General — open to EVERY registered voter:**
- The Republican primary winner **vs. Hardingham (DEM) vs. a blank write-in line** for Paradiso.
- Paradiso's **name will not be printed.** A voter choosing her must physically write her name on the blank line. Verbatim: *"a space will be left on the ballot where their name can be written."*
- Because this race is genuinely contested in November, **this is the election that decides District 2** — unlike races that are settled in August.

> **For the guide's write-in explainer:** the rule in "The rule" above is real, correct, and worth teaching — a lone write-in genuinely can lock every NPA voter out of a race that would otherwise be universal. But **do not illustrate it with County Commission District 2**, where it does not apply. Check the 2026 field for a race where *all* printed candidates share one party AND a write-in qualified; that is where the effect actually bites. (For contrast, **District 4** — Marc Jaffe (DEM) vs. Deryl Byron Loar (REP), both qualified, no write-in — has no August contest at all and goes straight to November.)

## 10. CURE PROCESS, PROVISIONAL BALLOTS, ACCESSIBILITY, SPECIAL SITUATIONS

### Signature cure — mail ballots
Deadline: **no later than 5:00 PM on the 2nd day after the election** (= **Aug 20** for the Primary, **Nov 5** for the General).
Two failure modes, one identical fix:
- **Signature mismatch** — signature on the return envelope doesn't match your record.
- **Forgot to sign** the return envelope.

Both require, by the deadline: (1) a completed **Vote-by-Mail Ballot Cure Affidavit, Form DS-DE 139** (PDF in English and Spanish on the SOE page), **AND** (2) **a copy of your identification** (acceptable forms are listed on the affidavit itself). The SOE will notify you if your ballot needs curing — but tracking your ballot yourself is faster than waiting.
Source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/election_information/vote_by_mail_ballot/vote_by_mail_cure_signature_mismatch_forgot_to_sign.php

### Signature cure — provisional ballots
Same standard and same **5:00 PM, 2nd day after the election** deadline: provide a copy of your identification.
Source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/i_want_to/track_my_provisional_ballot.php

### Provisional ballots — your rights
If you vote provisionally you **must be given a written notice of rights** stating:
- You may bring further evidence of eligibility to the SOE **until 5:00 PM of the 2nd day after the election**.
- **If you voted provisionally solely for lack of ID, you need bring nothing further** — it counts if you were in the right precinct and your signature matches.
- If your personal identifying number couldn't be verified, you may supply a copy of the card bearing that number **in person, by fax, email, or mail**, by 5:00 PM of the 2nd day after the election.
- Instructions for finding out afterward whether your provisional ballot counted, and if not, why.
- **If this is a primary, contact the SOE immediately** to confirm you're registered and able to vote in the General.

**Always vote in the precinct matching your legal residence** — wrong-precinct provisionals are the most common way a provisional fails.
Source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/i_want_to/track_my_provisional_ballot.php

### Accessibility
- **Assistance marking your ballot:** verbatim, *"If you need assistance in marking your ballot, please inform the poll worker."* Stated on both the Election Day and Early Voting pages.
- **Accessible voting equipment:** guaranteed under HAVA, which the SOE page confirms requires *"states to provide a way for those with impaired vision or hearing to cast a secret ballot"* and *"states to make polling locations and voting machines accessible to voters with physical disabilities."* The county uses **ES&S** voting systems. Sources: [HAVA](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/outreach_education/help_america_vote_act_hava.php), [Our Voting System](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/outreach_education/our_voting_system.php)
- **Mail ballot help:** a **designee for a voter with a disability** may request and pick up a ballot on the voter's behalf (photo ID + written authorization required).
- **Signature updates for changing signatures:** the SOE explicitly invites voters *"with physical disabilities or those affected by disease"* who have trouble maintaining a consistent signature to file an updated signature — do this **before** you mail a ballot, not after it's rejected. Submit a Florida Voter Registration Application by mail or in person. Source: [Register / Update](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/voter_registration/register_to_vote_update_your_voter_record.php)
- **Website accessibility:** SOE commits to Section 508 and WCAG 2.0 A/AA; accessibility contact (772) 226-4700.
- ⚠️ **CURBSIDE VOTING — UNCONFIRMED.** No SOE page I reviewed describes a curbside/drive-up procedure or the specific model of accessible ballot-marking device. Florida law provides for assistance to voters unable to enter a polling place, but I could not source the county's procedure. **Call (772) 226-4700 in advance.**

- **Moved within Indian River County or elsewhere in Florida:** update your address **online at [RegisterToVoteFlorida.Gov](https://registertovoteflorida.gov/)**, **by phone at (772) 226-4700**, by email, by paper Voter Registration Application, or by signed written notice. Source: [Register / Update](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/voter_registration/register_to_vote_update_your_voter_record.php)
- Your **polling place is assigned by your legal address** — moving usually changes your precinct. Re-check it at [My Voter Information](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/voter_registration/my_voter_information.php) before Election Day.
- **Mail ballots are NOT forwardable by USPS.** If your address is stale, your ballot will not chase you. Source: [VBM FAQs](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/election_information/vote_by_mail_ballot/faqs.php)
- You may update your voter record **at any time** — address, name, party, signature. Only the *party* change carries the 29-day cutoff.

### Students
Florida has no student-specific registration rule. A student who is a U.S. citizen, a Florida resident, and 18+ registers like anyone else; **pre-registration is allowed from age 16.** The practical issue is address: a student living away from their registered address should either (a) request a **mail ballot with the campus/away mailing address on file** (remember: not forwardable), or (b) come home to vote their assigned precinct. **Student identification is on the accepted photo-ID list** (§9). Source: [Register / Update](https://www.voteindianriver.gov/voter_registration/register_to_vote_update_your_voter_record.php)

### Military and overseas (UOCAVA)
Source: https://www.voteindianriver.gov/election_information/vote_by_mail_ballot/military_overseas.php
- **Who's covered:** U.S. citizens 18+ who are active duty Uniformed Services, Merchant Marine, or commissioned corps of the Public Health Service or NOAA; **their eligible family members**; and **U.S. citizens residing outside the United States.**
- **Federal Post Card Application (FPCA)** — the single form for both registering and requesting a ballot.
  - **Registering via FPCA:** must be **mailed** (signed) to the SOE **no later than 29 days before the election.**
  - **Already registered, requesting a ballot via FPCA:** may be **mailed, emailed, or faxed** to the SOE.
- **Federal Write-In Absentee Ballot (FWAB)** — emergency backup ballot if your state ballot never arrived in time. Obtain at [FVAP.gov](https://www.fvap.gov/).
- **Track your ballot** with the same mail-ballot tracker (§8).
- Resources: **[fvap.gov](https://www.fvap.gov/)** (Federal Voting Assistance Program), **[eac.gov](https://www.eac.gov/)** (U.S. Election Assistance Commission).
- ⚠️ **UNCONFIRMED:** whether Florida extends the *receipt* deadline for overseas ballots beyond 7 PM Election Day (some states allow a post-election window for UOCAVA). The SOE page does not state one. **Do not assume extra time — call (772) 226-4700.**
