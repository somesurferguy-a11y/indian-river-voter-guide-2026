## Office overview

Florida's governor is the state's chief executive, serving a 4-year term (2-term limit), and sits atop the Florida Cabinet alongside the Attorney General, CFO, and Commissioner of Agriculture. The governor appoints agency heads, judges (subject to retention), and fills vacancies; signs or vetoes legislation (including line-item budget vetoes); and, together with the Cabinet, oversees clemency, state lands, and other constitutional functions. The 2026 race is open because incumbent Ron DeSantis (R) is term-limited after two terms (2019–2027).
Source: [Ballotpedia, "Florida gubernatorial and lieutenant gubernatorial election, 2026," accessed 2026-08-25](https://ballotpedia.org/Florida_gubernatorial_and_lieutenant_gubernatorial_election,_2026)

## REPUBLICAN NOMINEE: Byron Donalds

### Biography

- Born Oct. 28, 1978; raised by a single mother in the Crown Heights neighborhood of Brooklyn, New York, alongside two sisters. His mother lost her city job when he was in middle school; his grandmother helped pay tuition for a Catholic high school. Moved to Florida for college.
 Source: [Time, "Who Is Byron Donalds, the Trump-Backed Favorite for [Florida Governor]," Aug. 18, 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/08/18/byron-donalds-congressman-trump-republican-governor-florida/)
- Lives in Naples, FL, with wife Erika Donalds (a school-choice activist and former Collier County School Board member) and their three children. Background in finance/banking/insurance prior to politics.
 Source: [Time, Aug. 18, 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/08/18/byron-donalds-congressman-trump-republican-governor-florida/)
- Florida House of Representatives, District 80, 2016–2020; elected to U.S. House (FL-19, Southwest Florida/Naples-Fort Myers area) in 2020, re-elected 2022 and 2024 (third term at time of gubernatorial run). Was on Donald Trump's 2024 vice-presidential shortlist; widely described as one of the most prominent Black Republicans in the country.
 Source: [Time, Aug. 18, 2026](https://time.com/article/2026/08/18/byron-donalds-congressman-trump-republican-governor-florida/); [Ballotpedia News, Aug. 20, 2026](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/08/20/byron-donalds-defeated-10-other-candidates-in-the-republican-primary-for-governor-of-florida-on-aug-18/)

### Running mate

- Selected Florida state Sen. Bryan Avila (age 42) as his Lt. Governor pick, announced at a Freedom Tower event in Miami on or about Aug. 25, 2026. Avila, born and raised in Hialeah, represented Cuban-majority Miami-area districts in the Florida House (2014–2022) and Florida Senate (2022–2026); is a Florida Army National Guard veteran.
 Source: [CBS Miami, "Florida Republican gubernatorial candidate Byron Donalds selects state Sen. Bryan Avila as running mate," Aug. 25, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/florida-republican-byron-donalds-gubernatorial-candidate-lieutenant-governor-running-mate-2026/); [WGCU, Aug. 25, 2026](https://www.wgcu.org/government-politics/2026-08-25/byron-donalds-picks-state-sen-bryan-avila-as-running-mate)

### Policy positions (with direct quotes)

**Property taxes / Amendment 3:** Amendment 3, on the Nov. 2026 statewide ballot, would raise the homestead exemption to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028. Donalds supports it and wants to go further regardless of its outcome: "Whether Amendment 3 passes or fails, we are going to reform property taxes in our state with a goal of eliminating homestead property taxes once and for all." If Amendment 3 fails, he says "we're going to bring that issue back through our Tax and Budget Reform Commission next year with the goal of eliminating homestead property taxes." He wants relief targeted toward seniors and working families.
 Source: [WLRN, "Byron Donalds promotes his economic plans for Florida, defends Trump's record," Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-21/byron-donalds-promotes-his-economic-plans-for-florida-defends-trumps-record); [Yahoo News/Newsweek aggregation, "Byron Donalds to Face David Jolly in Florida Governor's Race: What They Say on Property Taxes"](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/byron-donalds-face-david-jolly-155152696.html)

**Property insurance:** Wants to address high premiums through tort reform and by "supercharg[ing] some of our home hardening programs" for properties built before 1992 (pre-Hurricane Andrew building codes). Has separately promised insurance-rate cuts as the race pivots to the general election.
 Source: [WLRN, Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-21/byron-donalds-promotes-his-economic-plans-for-florida-defends-trumps-record); [WPTV, "Byron Donalds promises insurance cuts, defends data centers as race pivots to November"](https://www.wptv.com/news/state/donalds-promises-insurance-cuts-defends-data-centers-as-race-pivots-to-november)

**Housing/growth/development:** Wants to reduce construction permitting costs, which he claims add 20–30% to building expenses: "modernize that entire process, shrink those timelines down." His official platform also commits to infrastructure that will "not only keep up, but also be designed to meet the current and future needs of the state" as Florida grows.
 Source: [WLRN, Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-21/byron-donalds-promotes-his-economic-plans-for-florida-defends-trumps-record); [ByronDonalds.com, "Issues," accessed 2026-08-25](https://byrondonalds.com/issues/)

**Data centers/AI:** Supports continued data-center development, calling it necessary infrastructure for AI, aerospace, medicine and government services, and warning that bans/moratoriums would push investment and jobs elsewhere: "There ain't no bans coming." Proposes safeguards rather than prohibition — closed-loop water-cooling systems, protections for residential utility customers, and distance restrictions from homes — and argues Florida's grid has roughly five gigawatts of "excess power," enough to accommodate about five data centers. Has received "dozens" of campaign contributions from AI companies, per reporting.
 Source: [Florida Politics, "'There ain't no bans coming': Byron Donalds says critics can't stop AI data centers"](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/812893-there-aint-no-bans-donalds/); [PBS/WUSF coverage cross-referenced, Aug. 2026]

**Education:** Proposes "customized career roadmaps" starting in 6th grade with individualized curricula and real-world work experience; a "Healthy Florida Healthy Families" initiative to "remove ultra-processed food, artificial dyes, and harmful additives out of Florida school meals"; platform also commits to "keeping radical woke ideology out of schools."
 Source: [FOX 13 Tampa Bay, "Florida 2026 primary: Where governor candidates stand on property taxes, immigration, housing"](https://www.fox13news.com/news/florida-2026-primary-where-governor-candidates-stand-property-taxes-immigration-housing); [ByronDonalds.com/issues](https://byrondonalds.com/issues/)

**Environment/water:** Official platform pledges to "complete Everglades Restoration, improve water quality, expand access to water supply, and protect our beaches and natural resources." **No Indian River Lagoon-specific statement was found for Donalds in this research pass** — flagged explicitly as a gap rather than assumed.
 Source: [ByronDonalds.com/issues](https://byrondonalds.com/issues/)

**Immigration:** Committed to "fighting illegal immigration" as a continuation of the Trump administration's agenda; platform language: "secure the border, keep our families and communities safe, end taxpayer handouts to illegals, and stop drugs from pouring into our country."
 Source: [ByronDonalds.com/issues](https://byrondonalds.com/issues/); [FOX 13 Tampa Bay policy comparison](https://www.fox13news.com/news/florida-2026-primary-where-governor-candidates-stand-property-taxes-immigration-housing)

**Economy/general:** Wants to "make Florida the nation's financial capital – diversifying our economy, creating jobs, and attracting new investment"; defends the Trump administration's economic record, citing wage growth and GDP figures, and defends administration foreign-policy/energy choices around Iran tensions ("if the Iranians had a nuclear weapon, that would put major geopolitical pressure on the United States").
 Source: [WLRN, Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-21/byron-donalds-promotes-his-economic-plans-for-florida-defends-trumps-record)

### Record / controversies

- **Dual-campaign controversy:** Donalds maintained an active U.S. House re-election campaign committee alongside his gubernatorial run for more than 18 months. He notified the FEC on Feb. 25, 2025 that he would not seek House re-election, then filed as a gubernatorial candidate and transferred $1.2 million to his governor's-race PAC on Feb. 26, 2025 — but his House committee continued raising ($738,092 since Jan. 2025) and spending (over $2.1 million, including $136,561.47 on security services and $36,000 on polling/research) money afterward. On Aug. 5, 2025, the FEC warned that contributions to the House account "must be refunded" since he was not an active House candidate; his campaign treasurer Bradley Crate responded the next day that Donalds "has made no announcement to that effect" regarding ending the House campaign — directly contradicting the February FEC filing. The campaign did not confirm he wasn't running for House re-election until Aug. 8, 2026.
 Source: [WUSF, "Byron Donalds kept House reelection campaign alive during run for governor," Aug. 20, 2026](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-08-20/byron-donalds-kept-house-re-election-campaign-alive-during-run-for-florida-governor); [WLRN, same date](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-20/byron-donalds-kept-house-re-election-campaign-alive-during-run-for-florida-governor)
- **STOCK Act ethics complaint:** In 2024, the nonpartisan Campaign Legal Center filed an ethics complaint with the Office of Congressional Ethics alleging Donalds and his wife failed to timely disclose more than 100 stock trades worth up to roughly $1.6 million in 2022–2023, potentially violating the STOCK Act's 45-day disclosure requirement. A campaign spokesperson said Donalds does not personally initiate trades (his broker has third-party authorization) and that the campaign was "working to reconcile any outstanding infractions."
 Source: [Campaign Legal Center, press release, "Rep. Byron Donalds of Florida Fails to Disclose up to $1.6 Million in Stock Trades"](https://campaignlegal.org/press-releases/rep-byron-donalds-florida-fails-disclose-16-million-stock-trades-campaign-legal)
- **Crypto/timing questions:** Donalds sits on the House Subcommittee on Digital Assets; reporting noted he and his wife purchased Bitcoin (up to $100,000) the same day he and other lawmakers sent a letter to the IRS seeking more favorable crypto tax rules, raising conflict-of-interest questions given his policy role. Donalds has said his investments are handled by a financial advisor and called the attacks "politically motivated."
 Source: [NOTUS, "Rep. Byron Donalds Pressed the IRS for Favorable Crypto Rules. His Wife Bought Bitcoin the Same Day."](https://www.notus.org/congress/byron-donalds-crypto-rules-bitcoin); [Miami Times, "Byron Donalds says his investments are above board, condemns attacks as politically motivated"](https://www.miamitimesonline.com/electionnewshub/byron-donalds-says-his-investments-are-above-board-condemns-attacks-as-politically-motivated/article_f5ff3c2d-b82f-4579-b2a4-2fcb427caf0a.html)
- **"Jim Crow" remarks (2024):** At a June 2024 pro-Trump campaign event in Philadelphia aimed at Black-voter outreach, Donalds said, per a recording published by the Philadelphia Inquirer: "You see, during Jim Crow, the Black family was together." He pointed to 1960s Great Society programs (food stamps, housing assistance, Medicaid) as having, in his framing, weakened Black family structure since. Congressional Black Caucus Chairman Steven Horsford said "Black people were not better off during the Jim Crow Era," and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries denounced the remarks from the House floor. Donalds pushed back on the characterization in a nationally televised exchange with MSNBC's Joy Reid, saying: "I never said that it was better for Black people in Jim Crow" — while maintaining that Black marriage rates were statistically higher during that era. Independent fact-checks (PolitiFact, Tampa Bay Times) found Democrats' "he said Jim Crow was better" framing to be an overstatement of what he literally said, while also finding his own comments "thorny in their own right" and his implied causal argument about Great Society programs weakly supported.
 Source: [NBC News, "Rep. Byron Donalds defends comments about Jim Crow"](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/byron-donalds-defends-comments-jim-crow-fiery-exchange-joy-reid-rcna155969); [PolitiFact, "Fact-checking Byron Donalds' 'Jim Crow' comments on Black families," June 10, 2024](https://www.politifact.com/article/2024/jun/10/fact-checking-byron-donalds-jim-crow-comments/); [Tampa Bay Times, same fact-check, June 10, 2024](https://www.tampabay.com/news/florida-politics/2024/06/10/fact-checking-byron-donalds-jim-crow-comments-black-families/)
- **On the positive/record side:** widely credited as a prominent national Black Republican voice, was on Trump's 2024 VP shortlist, and built the largest fundraising operation in Florida gubernatorial history to date; has not (as of this pass) been found to have any adjudicated ethics violation — the STOCK Act and dual-campaign matters are complaints/FEC warnings, not final findings of wrongdoing.

## DEMOCRATIC NOMINEE: David Jolly

### Biography

- Born Oct. 31, 1972 in Dunedin, FL. Attorney; nationally recognized television political analyst (frequent MSNBC contributor). Devout Christian, per his own description.
 Source: [BiographyBrief/PeopleCabal aggregation cross-referenced with Ballotpedia, "David Jolly," accessed 2026-08-25](https://ballotpedia.org/David_Jolly)
- U.S. House, Florida's 13th District (Pinellas County/Tampa Bay area), March 14, 2014 – Jan. 3, 2017; won a special election as a Republican, then lost his 2016 re-election bid to Democrat and former Gov. Charlie Crist.
 Source: [Ballotpedia, "David Jolly"](https://ballotpedia.org/David_Jolly); [GovTrack.us, "Rep. David Jolly [R-FL13, 2014-2016]"](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/members/david_jolly/412603)
- Left the Republican Party in 2018 (announced during an HBO appearance), citing opposition to President Trump; remained a registered independent for several years and built a national media profile as a prominent anti-Trump former-Republican commentator; registered as a Democrat in May/June 2025 and announced his gubernatorial campaign shortly after.
 Source: [Ballotpedia, "David Jolly"](https://ballotpedia.org/David_Jolly); [Yahoo News, "David Jolly registers as a Democrat, making moves toward Florida governor bid"](https://www.yahoo.com/news/david-jolly-registers-democrat-making-105900166.html)
- Prior to Congress and after, worked as an attorney and (per his own congressional-era biography and later news coverage) as a lobbyist; his U.S. Senate-campaign-era Wikipedia page was edited in April 2016 to remove references to his lobbying work, which he said presented a "public negative narrative" — see Record/controversies below.
 Source: [GovTrack.us](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/members/david_jolly/412603)

### Running mate

- Selected Gwen Graham as his Lt. Governor pick, announced June 2026. Graham (born Jan. 31, 1963, Miami Lakes) is the daughter of Bob Graham, the former Florida governor and U.S. senator; the Graham family developed much of Miami Lakes. She represented Florida's 2nd District (Tallahassee/North Florida) in the U.S. House, 2015–2017, sitting on the Agriculture and Armed Services committees. She ran for the Democratic gubernatorial nomination herself in 2018, narrowly losing to Andrew Gillum. She later served 2021–2025 in the Biden administration as Assistant Secretary for Legislation and Congressional Affairs at the U.S. Department of Education.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Gwen Graham," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Graham); [WLRN, "David Jolly wins Democratic nomination for governor," Aug. 18, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2026-08-18/david-jolly-governors-race-florida)

### Policy positions (with direct quotes)

**Property insurance/affordability:** Central campaign theme is "historic homeowner's insurance reform" and addressing what he calls Florida's affordability crisis: "Floridians are being squeezed by an affordability crisis." Has floated shifting some catastrophic/disaster risk coverage from the private market toward a state-assumed loss-coverage model, potentially funded via a new real-estate-transaction tax or reallocated tourism revenue.
 Source: [WTSP, "Who is David Jolly? Meet the Democratic nominee for Florida governor"](https://www.wtsp.com/article/news/politics/elections/who-is-david-jolly-democrat-florida-governor-2026-race/67-9baa25b9-1a5d-4da0-ae1c-0168ff555583); [Washington Examiner, data-center/affordability aggregation, 2026]

**Property taxes / Amendment 3:** Opposes Amendment 3 (the homestead-exemption-expansion ballot measure Donalds supports), arguing local communities should retain control over tax exemption policy rather than have it set by state constitutional mandate.
 Source: [Yahoo News/Newsweek aggregation, "Byron Donalds to Face David Jolly in Florida Governor's Race: What They Say on Property Taxes"](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/byron-donalds-face-david-jolly-155152696.html)

**Data centers:** Calls for a statewide data-center moratorium while Florida studies environmental impacts, long-term economic effects, and privacy/security concerns tied to AI infrastructure — a direct contrast with Donalds, who opposes any ban. Some Florida counties have already enacted local moratoria.
 Source: [Florida Politics, "David Jolly calls for a halt to data centers, betting Byron Donalds is vulnerable"](https://floridapolitics.com/archives/808692-david-jolly-calls-for-data-center-moratorium/); [Washington Examiner, "As data centers sprout across the US, gubernatorial hopefuls look to ease voter fears"](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/campaigns/state/4673517/data-center-moratorium-governor-candidates-centers-should-pay-consensus/)

**Education:** Campaign centers on a "renaissance" in public education — reinvestment in community public schools and higher teacher pay, citing Florida's ranking as "near-last in teacher pay" among states as a crisis-level problem.
 Source: [FOX 13 Tampa Bay, "Florida 2026 primary: Where governor candidates stand on property taxes, immigration, housing"](https://www.fox13news.com/news/florida-2026-primary-where-governor-candidates-stand-property-taxes-immigration-housing)

**Immigration/Alligator Alcatraz:** Called the Everglades immigration-detention facility known as "Alligator Alcatraz" a "callous political stunt" and supports closing it and returning the land to Miami-Dade County. Says this doesn't make him soft on crime: "if you violate the law in Florida, you should be held accountable regardless of where you were born."
