Research compiled 2026-08-25 (one week after the Aug. 18 primary). **SUMMARY: Incumbent CFO Blaise Ingoglia (R), who was appointed to the office by Gov. Ron DeSantis in July 2025, won the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary over Frank Collige, roughly 968,183–610,837 votes (61%–39%). Former state Sen. This file documents full profiles for Ingoglia and Taddeo and basic bio/finance/primary-status information for the two eliminated candidates, Collige and Ford, per guide standards.

## Office overview

The Chief Financial Officer is one of three statewide-elected members of the Florida Cabinet (with the Governor, Attorney General, and Commissioner of Agriculture). The CFO heads the Department of Financial Services (DFS), which regulates insurance solvency and agent licensing, houses the State Fire Marshal, oversees the State Treasury, runs the Division of Consumer Services (financial/insurance complaints), investigates insurance and workers'-comp fraud through the Division of Insurance Fraud, and serves as the state's chief accounting officer, auditing state contracts and payments. The last Democrat to hold the office was Alex Sink, 2007–2011.
Source: [WLRN, "Democrats Annette Taddeo and Earle Ford compete to face CFO Blaise Ingoglia in November," July 24, 2026](https://www.wlrn.org/2026-07-24/democrats-annette-taddeo-and-earle-ford-compete-to-face-cfo-blaise-ingoglia-in-november); [Miami Times editorial board endorsement of Taddeo, Aug. 14, 2026](https://www.miamitimesonline.com/the-miami-times-editorial-board-endorsement-annette-taddeo-for-floridas-chief-financial-officer/article_40858c33-376c-4a90-9687-653189f59800.html), which describes the office as overseeing DFS, regulating "important aspects of the insurance and financial industries," managing state finances, and serving as State Fire Marshal.

- Blaise Ingoglia is Florida's 5th Chief Financial Officer. He was **appointed** (not elected) by Gov. Ron DeSantis on **July 16, 2025**, and was sworn in **July 21, 2025**, succeeding Jimmy Patronis, who resigned as CFO after winning a special election to Florida's 1st Congressional District.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Blaise Ingoglia," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ingoglia); [Bay News 9, "DeSantis taps state Sen. This is characterized by multiple outlets as DeSantis "snubbing" Trump's pick.
 Source: [Yahoo News/syndicated, "Gov. ## REPUBLICAN NOMINEE: Blaise Ingoglia (incumbent)

### Biography

- Born Nov. 4, 1970, in Queens, New York City; attended Queens College and Brooklyn College without completing a degree.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Blaise Ingoglia," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ingoglia)
- Relocated to Spring Hill, FL in 1996; founded two companies, America One Mortgage and Hartland Homes (homebuilding). Reported net worth of $28.3 million as of a 2022 financial disclosure.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Blaise Ingoglia," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ingoglia)
- Entered politics in 2008, spending roughly $50,000 of his own money on a county-office campaign focused on property-tax relief. Elected Chairman of the Hernando County Republican Executive Committee (2009), then Vice-Chairman of the Republican Party of Florida (2011); State Committeeman for Hernando County by 2016.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Blaise Ingoglia," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ingoglia)

### Political career

- **Florida House (2014–2022, District 35):** Won the 2014 general election with 51% over Democrat Rose Rocco, independent James Scavetta, and Hamilton R. Hanson.
- **Chair, Republican Party of Florida (2015–2019):** Ousted incumbent Chair Leslie Dougher shortly after his 2014 House win; reelected Jan. 2017 over Christian Ziegler. Appeared with President Trump in the Oval Office, May 2017.
- **House legislative record:** Co-sponsored efforts to restrict mail-in voting after 2020, saying of alleged fraud "I don't know, but I'm sure it was going on" without providing evidence. Pushed anti-"deplatforming" legislation targeting tech companies that notably exempted Walt Disney World's parent company from coverage.
- **Florida Senate (2022–2025, District 11):** Succeeded Wilton Simpson; won the 2022 general election with 75% over Green Party nominee Brian Moore (no Democrat contested that seat).
- **"Ultimate Cancel Act" controversy (Feb. 2023):** Filed SB 1248, which would have canceled the registration of any political party that "historically supported slavery or involuntary servitude" — aimed at eliminating the Florida Democratic Party. Ingoglia called it satire of "cancel culture," saying it would be "hypocritical not to cancel the Democrat Party" given the party's pre-Civil War history. The bill did not advance.
- **2024:** Sponsored legislation to abolish local civilian police review boards.
 Source (all above): [Wikipedia, "Blaise Ingoglia," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ingoglia)

### As CFO (2025–present) — record and controversies

- Appointed by DeSantis July 16, 2025; sworn in July 21, 2025 (see Incumbency background section above for the Trump/Gruters context).
- Began a statewide "Florida Department of Government Efficiency" (state-level DOGE) speaking tour with DeSantis starting July 2025.
 Source: [Wikipedia, "Blaise Ingoglia," accessed 2026-08-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaise_Ingoglia)
- **Fraud-enforcement record (a core campaign selling point):** As of mid-2026, Ingoglia's office reported CID (Criminal Investigations Division) investigators had made 1,219 arrests totaling $38 million in restitution since his July 2025 appointment; the Bureau of Insurance Fraud specifically reported 634 arrests over the prior year. DFS press releases through 2026 document a steady stream of individual insurance-fraud arrests (e.g., a Feb. 17, 2026 arrest of a licensed agent for selling fraudulent policies; an Aug. 20, 2026 arrest in a nearly $382,000 life-insurance fraud scheme; a July 2026 $159,000 fraudulent-claims arrest).
 Source: [MyFloridaCFO.com press release archive, various 2026 dates](https://myfloridacfo.com/news/pressreleases/); [CBS12, "Florida CFO Ingoglia touts 1,219 financial fraud arrests, defends end of Haitian TPS"](https://cbs12.com/news/local/florida-cfo-blaise-ingoglia-touts-1219-financial-fraud-arrests-defends-end-of-haitian-tps-temporary-protected-status-immigration-enforcement-illegal-workforce-crime-news)
- **Insurance-industry accountability rhetoric:** Ingoglia says of his approach: "I am not here to protect the profits of insurance companies... What I am here to do is protect you, the policyholders." He cites having "levied millions of dollars in fines to insurance companies for not doing what they are supposed to be doing."
 Source: [ClickOrlando 2026 Voter's Guide, CFO Republican primary, July 31, 2026](https://www.clickorlando.com/voters-guide/2026/07/31/2026-voters-guide-florida-chief-financial-officer-race-republican-primary/)
- **NEGATIVE ITEM — Free-speech/armed-agents controversy (postcard case, Oct. 2025, reported July 15, 2026 by Bay News 9):** A Largo resident, James O'Gara, mailed Ingoglia a postcard reading "You lack values" in fall 2025. Within four minutes of the CFO's office receiving it, Col. Simon Blank requested a "threat assessment," writing: "Can you have someone look into the name and do a threat assessment." Two armed DFS agents subsequently visited the O'Gara home in October 2025; the recorded encounter shows agents reassuring O'Gara he "wasn't in trouble" and finding "No credible threat directed towards the CFO." O'Gara's wife, Cathy O'Gara, called the visit abnormal: "this is not normal in America, to send the police for someone saying something like 'You lack values.'" Ingoglia defended the response: "Every elected official, especially those who are outspoken, are unfortunately targets for people that do not believe the same as you," while acknowledging officials "should always be better safe than sorry, but at the same time also protect free speech." After the story became public, 341 additional people reportedly mailed similar postcards to Ingoglia in a form of protest.
 Source: [Bay News 9, "Why Blaise Ingoglia's office sent agents to a Largo home," July 15, 2026 (updated July 17, 2026)](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/07/15/armed-agents-largo-home-protest)

A DFS spokeswoman, Sydney Booker, argued one such missing item was "different from a contract," calling it merely a purchase order not subject to the same disclosure rule. The article also noted that CDR Enterprises — a company owned by major DeSantis donors Carlos Duart and Tina Vidal-Duart that has received over $537 million in no-bid emergency state contracts — donated $100,000 to the Friends of Blaise Ingoglia political committee, an appearance-of-conflict pairing the article draws attention to (not an adjudicated finding of wrongdoing).

- Consistent DeSantis ally throughout his House, Senate, and CFO tenure, implementing DeSantis-aligned initiatives.

### Policy positions (2026 campaign)
