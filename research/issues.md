## ISSUE 3 — COUNTY BUDGET AND THE SHERIFF
*(placed first because the user asked for figure verification; other issues follow in order)*

### Background

Under Florida law (Ch. 30 and Ch. 129, Fla. Stat.), an elected sheriff submits a budget request to the Board of County Commissioners. If the commission reduces it, the sheriff may appeal to the **Florida Administration Commission** (the Governor and Cabinet) — a rare, statutorily-provided remedy. Indian River County's sheriff pursued both that administrative appeal and a circuit-court lawsuit.

**Settlement terms (VERIFIED FACT):**
- County provides an **additional $3.5 million** appropriation to the Sheriff's Office for FY2025-26.
- Funding split: **~31% general fund, ~69% Municipal Services Taxing Unit (MSTU)**.
- Both parties agree to a **$4 million increase** in the Sheriff's FY2026-27 budget; the Sheriff agrees **not to appeal** if FY26-27 is funded at that level.
- Flowers agrees to **dismiss both the circuit court case and the administrative appeal** before the Florida Administration Commission.
- Flowers agrees **not to seek further FY2025-26 budget amendments except in an emergency**.
- Total settlement commitment across the two years: **$7.5 million**.

### What the money was for, and the legal theory

**VERIFIED FACT:** The increase was primarily to fund **deputy pay raises — a base salary of $60,000/year** — plus staffing shortages and operational needs. ([WPTV](https://www.wptv.com/news/treasure-coast/region-indian-river-county/funding-feud-continues-between-indian-river-county-and-sheriff-over-14-6m-request))

**VERIFIED FACT (the legal claim):** Flowers' suit alleged the County Commission **violated state law in the budget approval process** by failing to properly discuss **which particular budget line items** it intended to reduce before adopting a scaled-back increase. This is a **procedural** claim, not a claim that the commission lacked authority to reduce the budget. ([WPTV](https://www.wptv.com/news/treasure-coast/region-indian-river-county/indian-river-county-sheriff-eric-flowers-sues-commissioners-over-budget-dispute); [TCPalm via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/indian-river-county-sheriffs-office-183525916.html))

**REPORTED — rhetoric:** Flowers publicly characterized the commission's action in terms of a "**defund the police movement**," blaming county leaders following the budget debate. ([WPTV](https://www.wptv.com/news/treasure-coast/region-indian-river-county/defund-the-police-movement-indian-river-county-sheriff-blames-county-leaders-following-budget-debate)) This was a **characterization by the Sheriff**, contested by commissioners who noted they had approved a $6.86M increase — label it as the Sheriff's framing, not fact.

**VERIFIED FACT — why the tone changed:** Coverage of the run-up to settlement notes both sides shifted toward cooperation following **a series of community tragedies, including the deaths of a deputy and two county employees**. ([WQCS, Apr 8, 2026](https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2026-04-08/indian-river-county-officials-move-toward-possible-settlement-in-sheriffs-budget-lawsuit))

### Named positions

- **Sheriff Eric Flowers:** "This funding provides critical support that allows us to continue delivering essential public safety services." Also: "I'm just happy that they were willing to come to the table and we were able to resolve this."
  Sources: [WQCS, Apr 14, 2026](https://www.wqcs.org/2026-04-14/indian-river-county-reaches-settlement-with-sheriff-eric-flowers-in-budget-lawsuit); [CBS12](https://cbs12.com/news/local/settlement-negotiations-to-begin-in-indian-river-county-sheriffs-lawsuit-against-county-commissioners)
- **Commissioner (Board Chairman at time of settlement) Deryl Loar** — himself a former Indian River County sheriff — supported the settlement: "The Board affirms its commitment to putting recent conflicts behind and moving forward in a cooperative, professional partnership." Loar characterized the deal as exceptional and **not precedent-setting**, and said the FY2026-27 figures are "set in concrete." (WQCS Apr 14, 2026; CBS12)
- **Commissioner Susan Adams** — expressed concern that the agreement could **establish problematic expectations for future budget cycles**. (CBS12)
- **County Attorney Jennifer Shuler** described the exchange: "In exchange for the county agreeing to provide the Sheriff an additional appropriation of $3.5 million…the Sheriff agrees to dismiss both his circuit court case…" (WQCS Apr 14, 2026)

### What remains unresolved / open questions

- **VERIFIED FACT:** The settlement covers only FY2025-26 and FY2026-27. **FY2027-28 and beyond are not addressed** — the same statutory conflict mechanism remains available to any sheriff after the capped year.
- **UNVERIFIED:** No public source found confirming the total legal costs incurred by either the county or the Sheriff's Office in the litigation. This is a legitimate open question for candidates.
- **UNVERIFIED:** No source found detailing exactly how the $3.5M was allocated inside the Sheriff's Office (deputy pay vs. vehicles vs. detention).
- **Structural issue:** the ~69% MSTU share means unincorporated-area taxpayers bear most of the added cost — a recurring point of contention between the county and the municipalities (Vero Beach, Sebastian, Fellsmere, Indian River Shores, Orchid), which fund their own police departments.

### Range of positions (neutral framing)

1. **The Sheriff's framing:** rapid population growth, call volume, competitive deputy pay in a regional labor market (competing with St. Lucie, Brevard, Martin agencies), and detention/jail costs justify large year-over-year increases. Proponent: Sheriff Eric Flowers.
2. **The fiscal-restraint framing:** the commission is the constitutional budget authority and must weigh the Sheriff against all other county needs while holding the millage flat; a settlement risks inviting future appeals. Proponent: Commissioner Susan Adams (concern about precedent).
3. **The reconciliation framing:** the litigation itself was costly and corrosive; a two-year deal buys peace and predictability. Proponent: Chairman Deryl Loar.

## ISSUE 2 — PROPERTY TAXES

### County millage and budget — hard numbers

**FY2025-26 (current year, adopted Sept 2025) — VERIFIED FACT**
- Adopted total county budget: **$598,860,844** (~$598.9M), which WQCS reported as **$72 million less** than the FY2024-25 budget.
- General Fund millage: **3.5475 mills** — **6.69% above** the rolled-back rate of 3.3250.
- Aggregate millage: **6.1158 mills** — about **6.5% above** the rolled-back rate of 5.7442.
- Source: [WQCS, Sept 19, 2025](https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2025-09-19/indian-river-county-approves-598-9-million-budget-for-2025-26); [FY25-26 Budget Book (indianriver.gov)](https://www.indianriver.gov/Document%20Center/Services/Management%20&%20Budget/Annual%20Budget%20Documents/2025-26/25-26-Budget-Book.pdf)

**Important nuance for voters (VERIFIED FACT):** Indian River County has held its General Fund and MSTU **millage RATES flat**, but because taxable values rose, the rate is above the "rolled-back rate" (the rate that would produce the same revenue as the prior year). Under Florida law that is technically a **tax increase** and must be advertised as such. Holding the rate flat while values rise = more revenue.

**FY2026-27 (proposed, July 2026) — VERIFIED FACT**
- Proposed total budget: **$614.8 million**, a **2.7% increase**.
- General Fund and MSTU millage rates **unchanged for a 7th consecutive year**; Emergency Services District millage unchanged for a **6th** consecutive year.
- Countywide **taxable property value growth: 6.8%**, generating **$7.27 million** in additional property tax revenue.
- **State-mandated constitutional officer funding increase: 8.1%, about $7.96 million** — i.e., the mandated increase to constitutional officers (sheriff, clerk, property appraiser, tax collector, supervisor of elections) **exceeded** the entire new property tax revenue.
- County Administrator **John Titkanich**: "We just kind of focused on what the core needs were, and it resulted in us coming in at a budget that's 1.5 percent below the May CPI."
- New responsibilities absorbed: **mental health court services** (effective Oct 1) and **animal control** taken over from the Humane Society.
- Zero-based budgeting introduced for some departments; some capital projects delayed.
- Sources: [WQCS, July 8, 2026](https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2026-07-08/indian-river-county-proposes-614-8-million-budget-with-flat-tax-rate); [FY2026-27 Budget Workshop Book, July 8, 2026 (indianriver.gov PDF)](https://www.indianriver.gov/Document%20Center/Services/Management%20&%20Budget/Annual%20Budget%20Documents/2026-27/Budget-Workshop-Book-2026-27.pdf)

### Florida's property-tax elimination amendment (on the Nov 3, 2026 ballot)

**VERIFIED FACT:**
- Gov. Ron DeSantis called a **special session on property tax relief** and unveiled a **"Save Our Homes from Excessive Property Taxes"** proposal. ([Executive Office of the Governor, 2026](https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2026/governor-ron-desantis-announces-special-session-property-tax-relief-unveils-save))
- The vehicle is **CS/CS/HJR 203 (2026)**, "Elimination of Non-school Property [Taxes] for Homesteads." ([Florida House](https://www.flhouse.gov/Sections/Bills/billsdetail.aspx?BillId=82728))
- Legislative votes: **Senate 30-9**, **House 75-26**. ([Florida Senate, June 2, 2026](https://www.flsenate.gov/PublishedContent/Offices/President/6_2_26_Senate_Passes_Historic_Property_Tax_Cut_for_Florida_Homeowners.pdf); [CBS Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/florida-property-tax-cut-plan-november-ballot-desantis-homestead-exemption-2026/))
- Goes to voters **Nov 3, 2026**; needs **60% approval** to pass.
- Mechanics: raises the homestead exemption from **$50,000 to $150,000 in 2027**, then **$250,000 in 2028**; the expanded exemption does **NOT apply to school taxes**. By **2037**, all homestead property would be exempt from **non-school** ad valorem taxes.
- A new-resident restriction: anyone establishing Florida residency **after Jan 1, 2027** must maintain residency up to **five years** before receiving the increased exemption.
- **Statewide fiscal impact (Florida House staff analysis):** reduces annual revenue to **non-school** governments by **$4.6 billion initially, growing to $8.4 billion per year**.

**GAP — see "gaps" section:** I could not locate a published, Indian River County-specific fiscal impact estimate (dollar loss to the county general fund, MSTU, or the Hospital District) from an official county source. Candidates and voters should demand one. What *can* be said with confidence: because the amendment exempts **school** taxes but not county, municipal, hospital-district, mosquito-control, or fire/EMS levies, the burden of the cut falls on exactly the local boards on the 2026 ballot.

### Range of positions

- **Supporters** (Gov. Ron DeSantis; Florida House and Senate Republican leadership; Senate President's office): homeowners face unsustainable tax bills driven by assessment growth; homestead property should not be taxed to fund non-school government; school funding is protected by carve-out.
- **Opponents / skeptics** (Florida Policy Institute; Miami-Dade Democratic Party; some county and municipal associations statewide): the $4.6B-$8.4B hole must be filled by service cuts, higher sales/other taxes, higher fees, or shifting the burden onto non-homestead property — renters, businesses, and second homes. The Tax Foundation has published analysis of the structural design.
- **The local-government administrator view** (generalized, statewide; no IRC-specific quote located): counties with a large share of homesteaded value lose proportionally more; counties reliant on tourist/non-homestead value lose less.
- Source set: [Tax Foundation analysis](https://taxfoundation.org/blog/florida-property-tax-proposal/); [Florida Policy Institute, Amendment ballot language & local fiscal impacts](https://www.floridapolicy.org/posts/florida-property-tax-amendment-ballot-language-summary); [Miami-Dade Democratic Party](https://www.miamidadedems.org/eliminate_property_taxes_in_florida)

### Local context

- **VERIFIED FACT:** Municipalities within the county also raised taxes for FY2026; see [Hometown News, "Vero sets 2026 millage and budget"](https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/vero-sets-2026-millage-and-budget/article_d660756d-0dac-55b1-9fcc-6153f062bb4c.html) and [Blue Water Healthy Living / TCPalm reprint, "Taxes to increase in Indian River County as municipalities finish fiscal 2026 budgets"](https://bluewaterhealthyliving.com/news/national-news/florida/taxes-to-increase-in-indian-river-county-as-municipalities-finish-fiscal-2026-budgets/).

## ISSUE 1 — GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT

### Population — hard numbers (VERIFIED FACT)

- **1990: 90,208** residents → **2023: 167,781** residents. Projected **~200,000–210,000 by 2050** (sources vary: the county's USB study materials say "nearly 170,000 now, ~200,000 by 2050"; another summary cites 210,000 by 2050, a 25% increase).
- The Oslo Road / south-county **Study Area has grown ~25% since 2010**, faster than the county overall.
- Sources: [IRC 2030 Comprehensive Plan, Ch. 1 Introductory (indianriver.gov)](https://indianriver.gov/Document%20Center/Services/Planning-and-Development/Planning%20Division/Comprehensive%20Plan/Ch01-Introductory.pdf); [IRC Urban Service Boundary Final Recommendations Report, Nov 2024 (hosted by 1000 Friends of Florida)](https://1000fof.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/IRC_USB_Final_Recommendations_v6-consolidated-report-w-BoCC-Rec.pdf); [TCPalm via AOL, "Indian River County to hold six meetings to ask residents about urban-service boundary"](https://www.aol.com/indian-river-county-hold-six-193229883.html)

### The Urban Service Boundary (USB) — what it is and where it stands

**What it is (VERIFIED FACT):** The USB is a line in the county Comprehensive Plan separating land where the county will extend **central water, sewer, and urban-level services** (and therefore allows urban densities) from rural/agricultural land where it will not. It is the single most consequential growth-control tool the County Commission holds. **The current boundary was drawn in the 1990s and has rarely been changed.**

**Status (VERIFIED FACT):**
- The county commissioned a **USB study**, with a **Final Recommendations Report dated November 2024** including a Board of County Commissioners recommendation.
- The county held **six public meetings (three pairs — 11:30 a.m. and 6:30 p.m.)** at the North County Library in Sebastian, the Intergenerational Recreation Center in Vero Beach, and County Commission Chambers, to take resident input. Commission Chair **Susan Adams**: "This study will direct development for the county for years to come."
- A separate **Oslo Road Corridor Study** identified a **1.9-mile gap** in the boundary complicating growth along that corridor.
- **On May 20 (2025), the County Commission voted 4–1** to expand the Urban Service Boundary by **nearly two miles** along the Oslo Road corridor and simultaneously imposed a **one-year moratorium** on zoning and land-use changes in the area bounded by **5th Street SW, 98th Avenue, 13th Street, and 58th Avenue**, to allow staff to draft an **overlay district** with clearer development standards.
  - **Note / verification caveat:** WFLX dated the Oslo Corridor public-comment story **May 20, 2025**. Confirm at [ircgov.legistar.com File #25-0560](https://ircgov.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=7400684&GUID=57582E2E-8E0A-4E1A-87A7-A93FBA84CDDE) whether the 4–1 expansion vote was May 20, **2025** (the "25-" file prefix suggests 2025). A one-year moratorium from May 2025 would have **expired around May 2026** — a live question for 2026 candidates.
- A **new I-95 interchange** (Oslo Road) is the growth driver; the county has separately taken public input on land use near it.
- Sources: [Hometown News, "IRC to expand USB along Oslo Road"](https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/irc-to-expand-usb-along-oslo-road/article_5478bf8f-c587-5b98-bee7-1486b404acc7.html); [WFLX, May 20, 2025](https://www.wflx.com/2025/05/20/oslo-corridor-project-continues-indian-river-county-residents-voice-concerns-about-boundary/); [CitizenPortal.ai meeting coverage](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/3410028/Indian-River-County/Florida/Indian-River-County-commission-weighs-expansion-of-urban-service-area-near-Oslo-Road); [CBS12, May 29, 2024](https://cbs12.com/news/local/population-growth-may-lead-to-expansion-of-urban-service-boundary-in-indian-river-county-water-sewer-roads-meeting-residents-vero-beach-may-29-2024); official engagement site [inspire-engagement.com/indian-river-usb](https://www.inspire-engagement.com/indian-river-usb)

### Specific controversial projects / rezonings

- **Oslo Road corridor / I-95 interchange** — the central fight. Residents at the public meetings raised **traffic, environmental loss, and overdevelopment**; land-use preferences split between agriculture and industry **west of I-95** and parks/homes/medical **east of I-95**. Resident **Donna Keys** argued publicly that the community largely favors keeping the land agricultural. (WFLX May 20, 2025; Hometown News)
- **9.2-acre ag-to-residential rezoning between 66th and 71st Avenues (October 2025)** — the County Commission voted **unanimously** to rezone from agricultural to residential for a local homebuilder to build affordable housing, increasing allowed density from **1 residence to a maximum of 73 units**. ([Hometown News, "County approves rezoning ag-to-residential"](https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/county-approves-rezoning-ag-to-residential/article_3b2f5e55-f565-50a9-9a09-54e29d50773d.html)) — VERIFIED FACT. This is a useful case for voters: the same commission that restrained the USB approved a 73x density increase when the project was affordable housing.
- **Buc-ee's** — a large travel center approved in the county has been used in regional commentary as the marker of the growth turn; a TCPalm opinion piece framed Oslo Road as "the next growth domino." ([Yahoo/TCPalm opinion](https://www.yahoo.com/news/florida-shame-coming-buc-ees-090245651.html)) — the Buc-ee's approval is VERIFIED FACT; the "domino" framing is **opinion**, not fact.

### Workforce / affordable housing shortage — hard numbers

**VERIFIED FACT (WQCS, May 21, 2026):**
- Market rents in Indian River County start at **$1,700/mo (1BR), $1,900/mo (2BR), $2,300/mo (3BR)**.
- Affording a 2BR at the standard 30%-of-income threshold requires roughly **$76,000/year**.
- The proposal under discussion targets households at about **$55,000/year (≈60% of area median income)**, with projected savings of **$7,200–$10,800 per household per year**.
- Over **1,200 market-rate rental units** built recently were characterized in the discussion as serving higher-income retirees rather than working families.
- **Specific proposal:** a **seven-acre county-owned parcel at 1840 25th Street, Vero Beach**, for affordable housing serving households up to **80% of area median income**, financed with **low-income housing tax credits**.
- Named participants: **Bethany Fortunato**, Indian River Habitat for Humanity (advocate); **Deryl Loar**, County Commission Chair; City of Vero Beach officials. WQCS reported **no documented opposition** at that early stage.
- Source: [WQCS, May 21, 2026](https://www.wqcs.org/wqcs-news/2026-05-21/indian-river-county-to-discuss-affordable-workforce-housing-with-vero-beach)
- County housing programs (SHIP — State Housing Initiatives Partnership) at [indianriver.gov](https://indianriver.gov/services/community_services/state_housing_initiative_partnership_program/index.php)

### The Live Local Act and local control

**VERIFIED FACT:**
- The **Live Local Act** took effect **July 1, 2023**. For a **10-year period** it requires local governments to allow **multifamily and mixed-use residential by administrative approval** (i.e., **no public hearing, no local rezoning vote**) in any area zoned **commercial, industrial, or mixed-use**, provided at least **40% of units are affordable** to income-eligible households for at least **30 years**.
- **SB 1730 (2025)** amended and expanded the Act. In **January 2026, the Vero Beach City Council voted unanimously** to direct staff to fold SB 1730 into its ongoing single-family zoning rewrite. ([Hometown News, "Vero Beach updates zoning for Live Local rules"](https://www.hometownnewstc.com/news/indian_river/vero-beach-updates-zoning-for-live-local-rules/article_70137709-1754-55c1-a42d-0c8edfe1d7a9.html); [City of Vero Beach Live Local Act page](https://www.covb.org/655/Live-Local-Act))

**Why this matters to voters (neutral framing):** the Live Local Act moves a category of high-density housing decisions **out of the hands of the elected boards voters are choosing in 2026** and into administrative staff review. A candidate who promises to "stop" or "approve" such a project may have limited legal authority to do either.

### Range of positions on growth

1. **Managed-growth / boundary-holding.** Keep the USB tight, protect agricultural land west of I-95, require infrastructure (roads, water, sewer, schools) to be in place before approvals. Proponents: many residents at the USB workshops (e.g., **Donna Keys**); **1000 Friends of Florida** (which is hosting the USB report); the **Indian River Neighborhood Association** ([indianriverna.com](https://www.indianriverna.com/post/more-urban-service-boundary-education-big-sugar-lawsuits-and-more)).
2. **Accommodate-growth / build-the-housing.** Growth is coming regardless; refusing density drives up housing costs and pushes workers into long commutes from St. Lucie and Brevard. Proponents: **Indian River Habitat for Humanity (Bethany Fortunato)**; homebuilders; employers cited in WQCS coverage; the commission majority that approved the 73-unit rezoning unanimously.
3. **Corridor-specific / infrastructure-led.** Expand the USB only where infrastructure already exists or is funded — the Oslo Road interchange justifies a narrow, 1.9-mile boundary fix, paired with a moratorium and an overlay district so standards are set before entitlements are granted. This is essentially the position the **4–1 commission majority** adopted.
4. **Property-rights.** Landowners west of the line argue the boundary strands their land value; some favor broader expansion. (Referenced in USB study materials; **no single named local proponent located** — GAP.)

## ISSUE 4 — WATER QUALITY / INDIAN RIVER LAGOON

### Background

The Indian River Lagoon is a ~156-mile estuary spanning Volusia through Martin counties; Indian River County sits in its middle. Excess **nitrogen and phosphorus** from septic systems, fertilizer, and stormwater fuel algal blooms that block sunlight, kill **seagrass**, and — through seagrass loss — have driven **manatee starvation** events. It is simultaneously the county's largest environmental liability and its tourism/property-value asset.

### Septic-to-sewer — current status (VERIFIED FACT)

Indian River County runs a dedicated **Septic-to-Sewer (S2S) Program** ([ircs2sprogram.com](https://www.ircs2sprogram.com/); county page at [indianriver.gov/services/utilities/septic_to_sewer_(s2s).php](https://indianriver.gov/services/utilities/septic_to_sewer_(s2s).php)). As of the 2026 program site, **four active projects covering ~244 homes**:

| Project | Homes | Phase | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floravon Shores Phase 2 | 19 | Bid phase | Community meeting **March 31, 2026** |
| North Sebastian Phase 3 | 168 | Design phase | Community meeting **June 17, 2026** |
| Hobart Landing | ~53–57 | Design phase | **75% funded by an FDEP grant** |
| Wabasso Island | not stated | Procurement phase | — |

**Funding sources named:** Florida Department of Environmental Protection (FDEP) grants; **St. Johns River Water Management District** cost-share ([sjrwmd.com](https://www.sjrwmd.com/streamlines/cost-sharing-our-way-to-a-cleaner-indian-river-lagoon/)); county utility funds.

**GAP:** The program site does **not** publish a total program cost, a total count of septic systems remaining countywide, a completion timeline, or the homeowner assessment/hookup cost. Those are exactly the numbers voters need and should be requested from IRC Utilities. **Do not state a total program cost — none is verified.**

### Contrast: Brevard County's dedicated funding (for comparison, VERIFIED FACT)

Brevard County voters approved a dedicated **Save Our Indian River Lagoon** half-cent sales tax, which funds homeowner grants — **minimum $6,000 per property** (approved December 2025) and **up to $20,000** to upgrade a conventional septic to an advanced system. ([brevardfl.gov](https://www.brevardfl.gov/SaveOurIndianRiverLagoon/SepticSystemUpgrades); [homeowner grants page](https://www.brevardfl.gov/SaveOurIndianRiverLagoon/Grants/HomeownerGrants))

### Seagrass and manatees — hard numbers

**VERIFIED FACT (lagoon-wide, well-established):**
- Between **2011 and 2020**, widespread harmful algal blooms killed an estimated **89% of the estuary's seagrass**. Roughly **140,000+ acres of seagrass** were lost lagoon-wide during the bloom crisis.
- From **December 2020 through April 2022**, manatee starvation tied to that seagrass loss produced a federally declared **Unusual Mortality Event (UME)** along Florida's Atlantic coast, with **at least 1,255 manatee deaths**. FWC has since **closed** the East Coast UME.
- Sources: [FWC — Closed Manatee Mortality Event Along the East Coast](https://myfwc.com/research/manatee/rescue-mortality-response/ume/); [Save the Manatee Club — Algae Blooms and Seagrass Loss](https://savethemanatee.org/manatees/algae-blooms/); [Frontiers in Marine Science (2026), multi-decadal aerial survey of manatee abundance vs. seagrass die-offs](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1725777/full)

**VERIFIED FACT (recovery signal, 2026):** Parts of **Mosquito Lagoon** (the lagoon system's northern reach, in Volusia/Brevard) show notable seagrass recovery, associated with improved manatee body condition. Seagrass is beginning to recover in parts of the lagoon alongside water-quality programs. NOAA is running an estuary restoration effort in the system. ([CleanTechnica, Mar 23, 2026](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/03/23/noaa-is-restoring-a-pristine-estuary-with-help-from-a-lot-of-friends/); [Frontiers, 2026]; [Yahoo/USA TODAY Network, "Manatees were dying in record-breaking numbers. But that trend may be slowing down"](https://news.yahoo.com/manatees-were-dying-record-breaking-083000038.html))

**LOW-CONFIDENCE — DO NOT PUBLISH WITHOUT CONFIRMING AT FWC:** One 2026 source (a real-estate market blog, not a scientific or news source) states **Indian River County recorded 7 manatee deaths in 2025, down from 25 in 2021**. The direction of travel is consistent with the closed UME, but **verify against FWC's published county-by-county manatee mortality tables at myfwc.com before using.** Label **UNVERIFIED** until confirmed.

**Caveat for the guide:** most seagrass loss and recovery reporting is for the **northern lagoon (Brevard/Volusia)**. Indian River County-segment-specific seagrass acreage was **not** located — see gaps.

### 7A. THE INDIAN RIVER COUNTY HOSPITAL DISTRICT — arguably the sleeper issue of 2026

This is a **taxing district with an elected board**, and 2026 coverage suggests it is the most contested local fiscal story after the sheriff dispute.

**VERIFIED FACT:**
- The District was created **~67 years ago** by an act of the Florida Legislature to levy a property tax to pay for **indigent care** at the community-owned Indian River Memorial Hospital.
- In **2019, the Cleveland Clinic Foundation assumed responsibility** for most hospital-based indigent care, relieving the District of that obligation.
- The District **did not reduce its budget or tax rate** afterward. Its budget went from about **$14 million** to a proposed **$25.37 million for FY2026-27** — reported as having **doubled since FY2021-22**, with **$2–3 million annual increases** in recent years. (A July 2025 report had the proposed figure at **$23.9M**, up **$2.2M**.)
- **FY2025-26 adopted millage: 0.7450 mills** (74.5 cents per $1,000 of assessed value) — [Resolution 2025-01, irchd.com](https://irchd.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Resolution-2025-01-Millage-.7450.pdf).
- **Proposed FY2026-27 millage: 0.7450 mills**, versus a **rolled-back rate of 0.7120** — the difference generates about **$1.2 million** in additional tax revenue.
- The District now funds **24+ local nonprofit medical/social-service organizations**: free adult and pediatric primary care, dental, mental health counseling, drug/alcohol detox, home health nursing, hospice, OB/GYN care, physical and occupational therapy, and home-delivered meals for low-income residents.
- **Property Appraiser Wesley Davis** calculated that the state property-tax amendment would cost the District **$2.1+ million in FY2027-28**, another **~$2 million** the following year, and a **~16% total revenue decline**.
- Source: [Vero News, Aug 4, 2026 (published under a 7/30/2026 URL) — "Hospital District can't seem to curb its spending habits"](http://veronews.com/2026/07/30/hospital-district-cant-seem-to-curb-its-spending-habits/); [Vero Beach 32963, July 24, 2025](https://vb32963online.com/STORIES%202025/JULY%202025/VB32963_Hospital_District_Increases_Budget_Again_By_22M_Issue30_072425.html); [IRCHD Financials](https://irchd.com/financials/); [IRCHD FY2025-26 Final Approved Budget PDF](https://irchd.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/2025-2026-DISTRICT-BUDGET-Final-Approved-Budget-9-18-25-530PM.pdf)

**Criticisms reported (label REPORTED ALLEGATION / opinion — Vero News is an opinion-inflected local outlet on this beat):**

## WHAT THESE ELECTED BODIES ACTUALLY DO

*(Written for voters. Every taxing figure below is sourced; where a figure is not sourced it is marked as such.)*

### Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) — 5 members, 4-year terms

- **Elected countywide** but each commissioner must reside in a district. (Florida counties may use district or at-large voting; **verify Indian River County's exact method at [voteindianriver.gov](https://voteindianriver.gov/) before publishing.**)
- **What it does:** adopts the **Comprehensive Plan** and the **Land Development Regulations**; votes on **rezonings, land-use amendments, and the Urban Service Boundary**; runs county **roads, bridges, utilities (water/sewer), solid waste, parks, libraries, building department, emergency management, and fire/EMS** (through the Emergency Services District); adopts the **county budget**, including funding the constitutional officers (Sheriff, Clerk of Court, Property Appraiser, Tax Collector, Supervisor of Elections).
- **Taxing authority:** sets the **General Fund millage**, the **Municipal Services Taxing Unit (MSTU) millage** (services delivered only in the unincorporated area), the **Emergency Services District millage**, plus special assessments and impact fees. It does **not** set school taxes.
- **Money (VERIFIED FACT):** FY2025-26 adopted budget **$598.9 million**; FY2026-27 proposed **$614.8 million**. General Fund millage **3.5475**; aggregate millage **6.1158** (FY2025-26). Rates unchanged for a 7th year in the FY26-27 proposal.
- **Not its job:** schools, insurance rates, state roads (FDOT), the courts, or city services inside Vero Beach, Sebastian, Fellsmere, Indian River Shores, and Orchid.

### School Board of Indian River County — 5 members, 4-year terms, **nonpartisan** races

- **What it does:** hires and evaluates the **Superintendent**; adopts the district budget; sets attendance boundaries and decides school openings, closings, and consolidations; approves curriculum and instructional materials (including **library material challenges and removals**); negotiates the teacher and staff contracts; approves charter school applications.
- **Taxing authority:** levies the school property tax — the **Required Local Effort** millage set by the Legislature (the board has no discretion over it), plus **discretionary operating millage** and a **capital outlay millage (up to 1.5 mills)** for buildings, buses, and technology. Any additional operating levy or half-cent sales surtax requires a **voter referendum**.
- **Money (VERIFIED FACT):** approved **$237.9 million** preliminary FY2026 budget, down about **$1.3 million (0.5%)**. Enrollment **below 14,000**; buildings at about **72% capacity**.
- **Key constraint voters should know:** most district revenue is **state-controlled** through the Florida Education Finance Program (FEFP) and follows the student — so vouchers and charter enrollment move money out of the district automatically, without any local board vote.

### Indian River County Hospital District — 7 elected trustees

- **A special taxing district created by act of the Florida Legislature ~1959.** Trustees are **elected countywide**.
- **What it does today:** levies a property tax and **grants the money to local health-care providers and nonprofits** — free/low-cost primary care, dental, behavioral health, detox, hospice, home health, OB/GYN, therapy, and meals. Since **2019** the Cleveland Clinic has borne most hospital-based indigent care. The District also holds the community's long-term interest in the former Indian River Memorial Hospital assets/lease.
- **Taxing authority:** its own ad valorem millage, separate from the county and the school board, capped by its enabling act.
- **Money (VERIFIED FACT):** FY2025-26 millage **0.7450 mills**; FY2026-27 proposed **0.7450 mills** vs. a rolled-back rate of **0.7120**; proposed budget **$25.37 million**, roughly double FY2021-22.
- **Why voters miss it:** it is far down the ballot, races are often uncontested or low-information, and meeting attendance is very low — yet it is the third-largest property tax levy most residents pay.

### Indian River Soil and Water Conservation District — 5 elected supervisors, **nonpartisan**, unpaid

- **What it does:** a state-authorized district (Ch. 582, Fla. Stat.) that promotes **soil conservation, agricultural best management practices, water conservation, and natural-resource education**. It typically works through partnerships with the USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service, UF/IFAS Extension, and local growers, and runs education programs (e.g., speech/poster contests, envirothons).
- **Taxing authority: NONE in practice.** Florida soil and water conservation districts have **no ad valorem taxing power**; they operate on small grants, county contributions, and state pass-throughs. Budgets are typically in the **tens of thousands of dollars**.
- **Why it's on the ballot anyway:** state law makes supervisors elected offices. As of **2023 (SB 1078)**, Florida requires soil and water district candidates to be **engaged in agriculture** on land within the district — this narrowed who may run. **VERIFY the current eligibility statute text before publishing.**
- **Practical note for voters:** these seats have almost no budget but real convening influence on agricultural runoff and lagoon nutrient policy — which is why lagoon advocates pay attention to them.
- Listed among county governmental entities at [indianriver.gov/government/other_governmental_entities.php](https://indianriver.gov/government/other_governmental_entities.php).

### Indian River Mosquito Control District — elected board, independent special district

- **What it does:** surveillance and control of mosquito populations countywide — larviciding, adulticiding (trucks and aircraft), **impoundment management on lagoon-side salt marsh**, and arbovirus surveillance (West Nile, EEE, dengue). Its impoundment work directly intersects with **lagoon habitat management**, so it is an environmental agency as much as a nuisance-control one.
- **Taxing authority: YES — its own ad valorem millage**, separate from the county.
- **Money (VERIFIED FACT):** millage **0.2400** for FY2023-24 (published FY2021-22 rate was **0.2515**). In FY2021-22 the district took in about **$5.08 million** in revenue and spent about **$4.14 million**, with **35 paid staff**, **49 vehicles**, and one facility with 7 buildings.
- Sources: [Indian River Mosquito Control District budget page](https://www.irmosquito.com/budget); [FY24-25 draft budget summary PDF](https://www.irmosquito.com/files/e73c728bd/Option+1+Budget+Summary.pdf); [Florida Auditor General FY2023 financial statements](https://flauditor.gov/pages/specialdistricts_efile%20rpts/2023%20indian%20river%20mosquito%20control%20district.pdf); [OPPAGA review, September 2023](https://oppaga.fl.gov/Documents/ContractedReviews/Indian%20River%20MCD%20Final%20Report.pdf)
- **Note:** OPPAGA (the Legislature's own research office) conducted a **contracted review of the Indian River Mosquito Control District, final report September 2023** — a substantive, neutral document worth reading and citing.
