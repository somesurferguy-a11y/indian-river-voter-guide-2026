# Florida Primary Election Results — Statewide Races (August 18, 2026)

Researched August 25, 2026. Results below are unofficial/complete returns as reported by the Florida Division of Elections and corroborated by news outlets; all four primaries were decisive (double-digit margins) and were called on election night by AP and other outlets. No indication was found that any of these results are in dispute or subject to a recount. If the Florida Secretary of State's formal statewide certification (typically issued about two weeks after a primary) becomes available with different final numbers, this file should be updated.

## One-line summaries

- **Agriculture Commissioner (R):** Incumbent Wilton Simpson defeated Matt Taylor ("Matt the Welder") 68.9%–31.1%.
- **Agriculture Commissioner (D):** Joey Mendoza Atkins defeated Donald A. "Don" Prichard 60.3%–39.7%.
- **U.S. Senate special election (R):** Incumbent (appointed) Sen. Ashley Moody defeated Chris Gleason, Ernest "Ernie" Rivera, and Neelam Taneja Perry with 79.6% of the vote.
- **U.S. Senate special election (D):** State Rep. Angie Nixon upset Alexander Vindman 56.1%–43.9%, despite being outspent roughly 16-to-1.

- Florida Politics, "Wilton Simpson runs away with GOP nomination for Agriculture Commissioner, Democrat Joey Mendoza Atkins advances to General Election" (Aug 18, 2026) — reports 68.91% Simpson / 31.09% Taylor, "about 1.61 million ballots cast, according to the Division of Elections": https://floridapolitics.com/archives/814743-wilton-simpson-runs-away-with-gop-nomination-for-agriculture-commissioner-democrat-joey-mendoza-atkins-advances-to-general-election/

Note: the two sources' percentages match to within rounding (68.9%/31.1% vs. 68.91%/31.09%) and the vote totals are internally consistent.

### Democratic Primary

**WINNER: Joey Mendoza Atkins**

| Candidate | Votes | % |
|---|---|---|
| Joey Mendoza Atkins | 716,467 | 60.3% |
| Donald A. "Don" Prichard | 472,100 | 39.7% |
| **Total** | **1,188,567** | 100.0% |

Sources:
- Wikipedia, "2026 Florida Commissioner of Agriculture election": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Florida_Commissioner_of_Agriculture_election
- Florida Politics (same article as above) reports matching percentages (60.26% Atkins / 39.72% Prichard) but a total-ballot figure of "1.89 million" that is **not internally consistent** with those percentages (60.26% of 1.89M would be ~1.14M votes for Atkins, not the ~716K implied by Wikipedia's totals, and 60.26%/39.72% of 1,188,567 works out almost exactly to 716,467/472,100). This looks like a reporting error in the Florida Politics piece (likely a mixed-up figure from another race/total). Flagging this discrepancy for transparency — the Wikipedia/Division-of-Elections totals (1,188,567 total votes) are used above as the more internally-consistent figure.

## Methodology / caveats

- I attempted to pull results directly from the Florida Division of Elections results portal (https://results.elections.myflorida.com/). As of this research (Aug 25, 2026), the portal's standard election-selection dropdown only listed elections through "2024 Primary Election" — the 2026 primary was not selectable through the standard interface I could reach, and a direct query attempt returned an internal ADODB/ASP error. I was not able to retrieve a certified statewide summary page directly from results.elections.myflorida.com or dos.elections.myflorida.com for this cycle.
- In the absence of a directly-fetchable official state summary page, the numbers above rely on (a) Wikipedia's results tables for both races, which cite the Florida Division of Elections' candidate tracking system as their source, and (b) corroborating contemporaneous news coverage (Florida Politics, NBC News, CBS News, NOTUS, JTA, MyNews13, ClickOrlando). Where sources' vote totals or percentages diverged, the discrepancy is noted explicitly above rather than silently reconciled.
- All four races were non-competitive by primary-night margins and were called by AP/major outlets on or shortly after election night (Aug 18, 2026); nothing found suggests these outcomes are contested.
