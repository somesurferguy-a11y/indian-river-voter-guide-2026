## 1. DISTRICT GEOGRAPHY — WHAT CHANGED, AND WHY YOUR OLD DISTRICT NUMBER IS WRONG

### The headline for Indian River County voters

**[VERIFIED FACT] Indian River County moved from Congressional District 8 to Congressional District 9.**
Under the new Florida congressional map signed into law by Gov. Ron DeSantis in May 2026, Indian River County is now part of **Florida's 9th Congressional District**. It was previously in District 8.

> "District 9 was previously contained to Osceola County and parts of Orange and Polk counties, but in the new map, it stretches from the Orlando area … down through the middle of the state, taking in Indian River, Okeechobee, Highlands and Glades counties."
> — WUWF/WUSF/WGCU (Florida Public Media), May 5–6, 2026
> https://www.wuwf.org/florida-news/2026-05-06/a-breakdown-of-the-changes-for-floridas-new-congressional-map
> https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-05-06/breakdown-changes-for-florida-new-congressional-map
> https://www.wgcu.org/2026-05-05/breaking-down-the-changes-in-floridas-new-congressional-districts

**[VERIFIED FACT] New FL-9 composition:** parts of **Orange**, **Polk** and **Osceola** counties (the Orlando/Kissimmee end), running south through **Indian River**, **Okeechobee**, **Highlands** and **Glades** counties. It is a long, narrow north-south district spanning the peninsula's interior plus the Treasure Coast.

**[VERIFIED FACT] What happened to District 8:** Under the prior map, FL-8 included Indian River and Brevard counties plus roughly 2,800 Orange County voters. The new FL-8 **drops Indian River County** entirely and pushes west, adding roughly 160,000 more Orange County residents.
— Florida Public Media, May 5–6, 2026 (same URLs above)

### Partisan effect of the change

**[VERIFIED FACT] FL-9 flipped from Democratic-leaning to Republican-leaning on paper.** Under the old lines FL-9 was a seat where more than 51% supported Kamala Harris in 2024. Under the new lines it is territory where more than 58% voted for Donald Trump.
— Florida Public Media redistricting breakdown, May 5–6, 2026
— See also Florida Politics, "Redistricting shuffle: What's the state of play in Florida's congressional contests under Ron DeSantis' new map?" https://floridapolitics.com/archives/794448-redistricting-shuffle-whats-the-state-of-play-in-floridas-congressional-contests-under-ron-desantis-new-map/

**Why this matters for the voter guide:** the Aug. 18 Republican primary is now, on these numbers, the most consequential contest in FL-9 — a reversal of the district's prior character. Democratic incumbent Darren Soto is running in a district that voted for Trump by a wide margin.

### ⚠️ FLAG FOR VOTERS: conflicting information from the Indian River County Supervisor of Elections

**[VERIFIED FACT — discrepancy]** As of early August 2026, the Indian River County Supervisor of Elections website contained **inconsistent district information**: the **maps page still referenced the old Congressional District 8**, while the **homepage announced the change to District 9**.

**Voter guidance:** Do not rely on a county district map that may not have been updated. **Check your own sample ballot** — mailed to registered voters ahead of the election and available through the Supervisor of Elections — or your voter information lookup, which reflects the district you will actually vote in. If the map page and the ballot disagree, the ballot governs. Call the Supervisor of Elections office if in doubt.

*(Also note: Florida's Aug. 18 primary is **closed**. Only registered Republicans may vote in the Republican primary for FL-9. The registration book-closing deadline for the primary has passed as of this writing; voters who wish to change party affiliation for the November general should check current deadlines.)*

**[VERIFIED FACT] District population and full county list:** The redrawn District 9 covers parts of **Orange**, **Osceola**, **Indian River**, **Highlands**, **Okeechobee** and **Glades** counties plus a small southeastern slice of **Polk** County — roughly **769,000 residents**.
— Gary White, *Lakeland Ledger*, "7 Republicans vie for shot at Soto in revised U.S. House District 9," July 30, 2026. https://www.aol.com/articles/7-republicans-vie-shot-soto-091333000.html

**[VERIFIED FACT] Timeline of the map:** Florida's Republican-controlled Legislature approved the redistricting in **April 2026** at Gov. DeSantis's urging; DeSantis **signed it in May 2026**. Reporting describes the change as adding heavily Republican southern counties with lower Latino voter concentrations to FL-9.
— *Lakeland Ledger*, July 30, 2026 (URL above); Florida Public Media, May 5–6, 2026.

**[VERIFIED FACT] Early voting for the primary began Aug. 8, 2026; primary election day is Aug. 18, 2026.**
— *Lakeland Ledger*, July 30, 2026.

### Additional demographic effect — [VERIFIED FACT]

- **Roughly three-fifths of the old District 9's population carries over into the new District 9.** The new lines **drop some of the district's reach into Orlando and Kissimmee, and drop Poinciana entirely**, while adding Indian River, Highlands, Glades and Okeechobee.
- **The district's Hispanic share falls from 54% under the old lines to 41% under the new map** — FL-9 is **no longer a majority-Hispanic district.** This is the change at the center of the litigation described below.
— WLRN/WUSF, "How Florida's new congressional map is shaking up the 2026 election," June 15, 2026 https://www.wlrn.org/the-florida-roundup/2026-06-15/how-floridas-new-congressional-map-is-shaking-up-the-2026-election ; Inside Elections, "Florida House: Redistricting Boosts GOP prospects" https://www.insideelections.com/news/article/florida-house-redistricting-boosts-gop-prospects

### ⚠️ SECOND FLAG FOR VOTERS: the map is being litigated, but it WILL be used in 2026

**[VERIFIED FACT] The new map is under active legal challenge — and it is still the map for this election.** Voters should understand both halves of that sentence.

- **May 4, 2026:** Florida voters and voting-rights groups sued, arguing the new map violates the **Fair Districts Amendment** — the anti-partisan-gerrymandering provision Florida voters approved in 2010. At least **three lawsuits** were filed.
— ClickOrlando, May 5, 2026 https://www.clickorlando.com/news/politics/2026/05/05/3-lawsuits-now-filed-to-block-new-florida-congressional-map-after-redistricting/
- **May 26–27, 2026:** **Circuit Judge Joshua Hawkes**, a DeSantis appointee, **denied** the plaintiffs' request to temporarily block the map for the 2026 midterms.
— WUSF, "Judge rules Florida's new congressional map will remain intact ahead of midterms," May 27, 2026 https://www.wusf.org/courts-law/2026-05-27/florida-new-congressional-map-will-remain-intact-ahead-of-midterms ; Axios, May 26, 2026 https://www.axios.com/2026/05/26/florida-redistricting-map-desantis-judge
- **June 11, 2026:** The **Florida Supreme Court, 6–1, denied** the request for a temporary injunction — **without ruling on the merits**, saying it lacked jurisdiction to intervene while the case proceeds in the lower courts.
— WUSF, June 11, 2026 https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-06-11/florida-supreme-court-rejects-challenge-new-redistricting-map ; NBC News https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/florida-supreme-court-rejects-challenge-new-gop-drawn-congressional-ma-rcna349602
- Challengers have vowed to continue; reporting notes the case **may stretch into the 2028 cycle**. Common Cause Florida Executive Director **Amy Keith** said the groups would "continue our fight to protect the will of Floridians."

**Bottom line for the guide: [VERIFIED FACT] Indian River County votes in District 9 in 2026.** The litigation does not change that. It may change the map for future elections.

*(Even-handedness note: describe the lawsuits neutrally. No court has ruled on the merits. Outlets like Democracy Docket that call the map an illegal gerrymander are advocacy-aligned and should be attributed as such, not cited as neutral findings of fact.)*

For a district that includes both fast-growing Orange/Osceola and rural interior counties, this may be the most genuinely differentiating issue in the primary.

**Third pattern — a real gap in the public record:** despite Florida's **property insurance crisis** and **hurricane recovery** being top-tier statewide concerns, **only Jorge Martinez mentioned insurance**, and **no Republican candidate had a located position on hurricane recovery or offshore drilling.** Voters should press all seven Republicans on these.

**Fourth pattern — the clearest general-election contrast is on water and drilling.** Soto has a documented, committee-backed record on Everglades/Indian River Lagoon algal blooms and a stated position **against offshore drilling off Florida's coasts**. **No Republican in the field had a located position on offshore drilling at all.** For a district that now includes the Treasure Coast, that is a live question the eventual nominee should be asked.

**Fifth pattern — abortion and guns are essentially absent from the Republican primary's public record.** Soto's positions on both are documented; **not one of the seven Republicans had a specific, sourced position on either issue** in the material reviewed. This is a genuine hole, not an editorial choice, and the guide should say so rather than leave blank cells unexplained.
